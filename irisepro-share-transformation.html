PROMPT What the share should actually ask for

Three beats, not six questions. A good share communicates who they were being, what they did, and what possibility they invented out of taking part. Below: what is live today, what Sutirtha proposed on 15 Aug, and what this storyboard recommends.

Why these three beats, and in this order

Who I was being earns the next twenty seconds — another teacher or parent sees themselves and keeps watching. What I did makes it believable; without a specific act it is a testimonial cliché. What I invented is the only beat that moves anyone watching to do anything.

It also makes the video safe to post. The live task asks for a weakness; this asks for a shift they authored. Same honesty, opposite standing — nobody is exposed, because they are the one who did it.

The question that should not be dropped

Task 419's "what support, training or resources do you still require?" is genuinely useful — and it is an unusually clean commercial signal. A teacher naming what they still need is a teacher telling you what to offer them next.

It just cannot be in a video meant for Instagram. Bring it back as a private field on the same task, visible to the Yashodarshi and never to the public.

Task 251 puts a minor on camera. Of all the 21st tasks it is the most genuinely shareable — a real family, real emotion — and the hardest consent case in the catalogue. The guardian link_exposure consent covers the child posting; a parent describing a worry about a named child, published, is a separate editorial judgement worth making deliberately.
"Who were you being" needs its own translation. It is exact, but it reads as jargon to a teacher or a Class 6 child who has never met the distinction. The prompt must carry the sentence-completion form and two or three examples, or half the videos come back as "before, my school had no smart board."

What Sutirtha's example should actually do

Shoot it badly on purpose. Phone held in one hand, vertical, one take, real classroom or home behind her, one stumble left in. A polished studio piece teaches teachers that this needs production they don't have — and they won't record at all.

Hit the three beats visibly, pausing between them, so the structure is learnable by watching rather than by reading.

Keep it under 90 seconds — the example must obey the rule it is demonstrating.

Staff example ≠ peer example

Sutirtha is staff. Her video is a format demo — "this is the shape" — and that is genuinely useful for launch, because it can exist today.

But the thing that makes a teacher actually record is seeing another teacher do it. As soon as two or three Acharyas have submitted well, swap in a real one (with written consent) and label it "Anitha, Class 7, KVS Bengaluru". Social proof beats instruction.

Ideal end state: two rows — one demo, one peer.

What makes it seamless

One decision per screen. No scrolling past things that aren't yet relevant — the link field doesn't exist until there is something to link to.

Why this and not "share to Instagram" buttons

Instagram has no web share URL. There is no instagram.com/share?url=… and there never has been. You cannot pre-fill an Instagram post from a link, so a row of per-platform buttons cannot do what Sutirtha is asking for. The OS sheet can, because the video file travels with it.

LinkedIn ignores caption parameters too. share-offsite dropped title/summary; it reads Open Graph off the URL you hand it. Only WhatsApp and X genuinely pre-fill text.

Untested on your devices. iOS often drops the text field when a file is attached, which is exactly why the caption also goes to the clipboard in screen 03 — belt and braces. This needs a real device pass on one iPhone and one Android before it ships.
The collaborator invite cannot be automated. No share intent on any platform can add a collaborator — it is a manual in-app step. It can only ever be an instruction plus a scoring criterion, which is what you had already concluded in the DM.

Feedback

What landed, and what would make the next one stronger…
A · Score it as abilities — zero code
  • Task 419 already maps 52 abilities — 11 aptitude · 11 attitude · 14 communication · 16 execution.
  • link is already a first-class evidence mode, so nothing is flagged by adding it.
  • The judgement beats — owned a way of being, the act was specific, declared a possibility — land on existing dials: confidence, structure, initiative, uniqueness.
  • Checking instructions ride in deliverable_spec, which the evaluator already sees (evaluate-detail.blade.php:173). No code.
  • ⚠ Run php artisan tasks:verify-mode-coverage after authoring — a non-canonical ability key does not error, it silently becomes scoreable in every mode.
B · The checklist — small and new
  • Four booleans + a platform count. Objective facts, so no inter-rater drift.
  • Storage: a new JSON column on yashodarshi_evaluation_results. Do not reuse score_snapshot without checking what writes it first.
  • Must be per-task configurable, or the checklist appears on all 240 tasks.
  • It records; it does not compute. The score stays one authority — the ability dials — and the checklist disciplines the evaluator's judgement rather than competing with it.
  • This is what "marks deducted if they don't add the collaborator" actually needs: today there is nowhere for that fact to live.

Two constraints that bound anything built here

Only four pillars reach the scorecard. yashodarshi_evaluation_results has fixed aptitude_score / attitude_score / communication_score / execution_score columns feeding total_score. A criterion that maps to none of the four never reaches the learner.

Senior evaluators only. Challenge::pool() returns premium_4999 for tier 4999 and storeEvaluationResult does abort_unless($yashodarshi->isSenior(), 403). All three Acharya challenges are ₹4999 — so only senior Yashodarshis can score this task at all. Worth confirming there are enough of them before the 21st task lands for everyone at once.

  • Not a boolean. is_share_task invites is_reflection_task next; flag-accretion is how three renders end up disagreeing. A closed set names the question once.

  • App\Support\Task\TaskScreen is the codebase's own answer to this class of problem — "The ONE question every surface asks about a task, and its ONE answer", written after the dashboard card grew seven independent authorities.
  • TaskScreen owns state (live / submitted / blocked+reason). Kind is a different question, so it gets its own resolver rather than being bolted into that one.
  • One switch selects a bundle. Three independent flags would eventually produce a task with the stepper and no checklist, and nothing would catch it.
There is already a task_type column — and it's a zombie. enum('CareerRise','Sankalp'), retired in #68 ("nothing branches on task_type's value"), with code comments stating the column was dropped — yet it is still in the schema. Do not repurpose it: that resurrects a deliberately-killed field with the wrong enum. Adding a near-identical second type column beside it is the two-implementations smell. Drop task_type in the same migration that adds task_kind.

Three rollout traps

Default standard = zero behaviour change across all 240 existing tasks. Adding a column is expand-safe, so it ships with deploy.sh --migrate.

Tag the task, not the challenge_task pivot — task 419 is the same kind in all three Acharya challenges. And because task_snapshot stores task ids, the tag reaches learners already mid-journey (the same reason a text rewrite reaches them and a new task does not).

Submissions made before the tag exists carry no checklist data. The evaluator partial must render "not captured" — never empty checkboxes. Unticked boxes read as "they didn't do it", and an evaluator would score down on absent data.

The fork is the LAYOUT only. task-timer, task-nav-guard, task-telemetry and media-recorder stay shared partials — re-implementing the timer inside a bespoke view is how expiry behaviour drifts from the other twenty tasks.

Trap 1 — the gating task MUST have a deadline. TaskExpiry fires off expires_at. Disable the timer and the rescue never runs, no terminal row is written, and the scorecard is blocked forever. The deadline stops being time pressure and becomes the guarantee that the gate opens.
Trap 2 — an unallocated unit blocks it permanently. $yashodarshiId = $unit?->yashodarshi_id; if (! $yashodarshiId) return false; — no evaluator on the unit ⇒ no terminal row ever. Why a bonus task, not the 7th / 21st
  • Replacing the 7th costs 12 graded tasks. Measured: 12 distinct 7th tasks across 14 challenge slots — Picture Comprehension, First 100 Days as District Collector, AI Engineer for a Day… each needing a rewrite, a mode change and a re-authored ability map.
  • The 90-minute sprint makes an in-task share impossible — per-task slots are 90÷7 ≈ 12 min, inside a non-stop proctored sitting.
  • Measured cost of NOT excluding it from the sprint: 7 tasks → [12,12,12,12,12,12,12] vs 8 tasks → [10,10,10,10,10,10,10,18] — every real task loses 17%, and the bonus task's own 6h SLA is crushed to 18 minutes.
  • ⚠ A new task never enters a frozen task_snapshot — so it reaches nobody currently mid-journey. New units only, unless a snapshot backfill is scoped separately.
The badge — why the existing model can't hold it
  • Every badge today is a threshold on a pillar percentage — achievements.threshold (0–100) vs a tier-normalised domain score.
  • Brand Ambassador is earned by an act, not a score — and the share task deliberately has no rubric, so it contributes 0 to every domain and can never cross any threshold. The thing that makes it safe for the scorecard makes it invisible to badges.
  • The substrate already exists: student_achievement carries unlocked_at and is already written via attach() — but only from the dead Flutter API (Api/MobileStudentApiController:1263), which has no live consumer. The grant path works and nothing calls it.
  • So: one migration (award_type + event_key, domain nullable), grant from the live Blade path, display branches on type. One badge concept, two award paths, one surface — not a second badge system.

One copy point that matters

If the task gates but does not score, the learner has to be told so. "Unlock your scorecard" reads as "this is graded" — and a teacher who believes a rushed share will drag their marks down will either skip it or rush it badly. The screen must say it plainly: "One last thing — share your story to unlock your scorecard. This one isn't marked."

OPEN Three decisions this storyboard does not make

Each changes the build materially. None should be assumed.

1 · The 7-task journeys have no video task at all

Derived across all 31 challenges: every one of the 14 seven-task journeys has zero video tasks. Their 7th tasks are graded subject work — Picture Comprehension, The Silent Networker Challenge, Your First 100 Days as District Collector.

So "update the 7th task" would paste share instructions over an assessment task. The 21-task journeys all end in a reflection video and take this naturally; the 7-task ones do not end in anything. Either they gain a closing reflection, or the share ships to 21-task journeys only.

2 · Native sheet, or a public share page?

Everything drawn above is the native sheet — nothing becomes public, no consent changes, no retention changes. It gives Instagram and WhatsApp exactly what they need.

A public page with Open Graph tags is the only thing that produces a real preview card on LinkedIn and X, and the only thing that sends traffic back. It costs: a public copy of the video, a retention change (originals are deleted after evaluation today), a new consent for minors, and a takedown path.

3 · Where do the diagnostic questions go?

Task 419's "what weakness became clear" and "what support do you still require" are genuinely useful assessment signal. They just cannot be in a video meant for Instagram.

Keep them as a private second deliverable on the same task, move them earlier in the journey, or fold them into the rubric the Yashodarshi scores against — a product call, not a technical one.

4 · Existing learners mid-journey

You raised this in the DM: a catalogue edit may not reach learners already inside a unit, because a started unit's task set is frozen in its task_snapshot — that is deliberate, and it is what protects a journey from catalogue churn.

Editing task 419's text reaches everyone, since the snapshot stores task IDs. Adding a new task does not. That is the real argument for rewriting 419 rather than creating a 22nd task — and it needs confirming against the running box before anyone promises it to Sutirtha.
