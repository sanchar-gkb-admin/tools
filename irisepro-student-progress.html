Why “blocked on” beats a completion percentage

A single percentage hides the only thing that changes what you do. 4-of-7 because the learner stopped, and 4-of-7 because we have not graded, look identical — and need opposite responses.

Splitting the bar into evaluated and submitted-awaiting makes our own backlog visible in the learner's row, where it is embarrassing enough to act on.

Two actions, and they go to different people

Nudge evaluator reuses the mail already built for the Yashodarshi console — the same message, so an evaluator never gets two accounts of one deadline.

Remind learner should reuse the window-nudge that already exists (tasks:nudge-window), not a third reminder system.

Not on this screen: anything that mutates a learner's work. It reads and it messages. Re-enabling a task or moving a seat already has deliberate, guarded tools (reenable-media-tasks.sh) and should not become a button next to a progress bar.

The classifier is the whole feature

Everything else is a count. US · LEARNER · STALLED · COMPLETE is the judgement, so it belongs in one place with its own tests — the same shape as TaskScreen, which exists because seven surfaces once answered “what state is this task in?” differently.

Its closed set must include NOT STARTED as distinct from STALLED: 42 learners who never began is a sales problem, 12 who began and stopped is a product one.

Measured, from the 23-Aug audit

Of 69 paid learners with no scorecard, 57 had ≤1 attempt — they enrolled and never really started. Only a handful were blocked on us.

So on real data this screen is mostly a non-engagement report, and the “waiting on us” rows are the rare, urgent minority. Worth designing the empty-ish state honestly rather than assuming a busy board.
