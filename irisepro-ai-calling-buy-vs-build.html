1 · Reframing: cost-first, pre-revenue

"Cost is the deciding factor" changes the objective function, and it is worth being precise about what it changes it to.

For a pre-revenue startup, cost-optimal is not lowest headline rate. It is lowest cost of being wrong. Three consequences, and they drive everything below:

  • Prefer variable cost to fixed cost, always. A ₹5 lakh annual contract at 40 learners is not a discount, it is a bet on a volume you do not have. Pay-as-you-go at a worse per-minute rate is cheaper in expectation than a commitment at a better one.
  • Engineering time is your most expensive input, not your cheapest. It is the one resource you cannot buy more of, and every week spent on telephony plumbing is a week not spent on the product that creates revenue. Treating engineer-weeks as free is the classic way a "cheaper" build becomes the most expensive line in the budget.
  • "Cost-first" and "great support" are in direct tension, and you have already paid for that lesson. Superfone was the cheap option. Seed-stage vendors under-staff support first because it is the largest cost that does not show up in a demo. So the resolution is not "buy expensive support" — it is buy no commitment, so that bad support costs you a migration rather than a contract.

The decision rule I am applying

Minimise total cost to a working, compliant hot-lead call — licence + usage + engineer-weeks + the expected cost of switching if it fails — over the next two quarters, not the next two years. On that rule the answer is unambiguous, and it stays unambiguous even if Bolna's rate doubles.

5 · Per-stream agents: KAS aspirant vs class-5 parent

A real requirement with a real architectural answer — and one DPDPA constraint that must be designed in, not bolted on.

You are right that these are different conversations, and the difference is not tone — it is who is on the phone:

KAS / IAS aspirantParent of a class-5 student
Who answersThe learner — an adult, buying for themselvesThe guardian — buying on behalf of a child
MotivationSelf-efficacy, competitive edge, own careerThe child's future; often anxiety-led
Objection shape"Will this actually help me clear the exam?""Is this safe, age-appropriate, worth the money?"

The design: one agent per (stream × persona), all driven from your own data

YOUR LARAVEL BACKEND decides WHICH agent, and passes the context

  lead captured → resolve stream (kas · ias · jee · neet · board · marks · career)
                  resolve persona (self-buyer adult | guardian-of-minor)
                          ↓
  POST /call  { agent_id: "kas_adult_v3", context: { first_name, stream_label,
                exam_cycle, price_ladder, NEVER: child name, child task scores } }
                          ↓
  BOLNA runs that agent's prompt + voice + flow. You version the agent, not the code.

  7 streams × 2 personas = up to 14 agents. Start with 2 — kas_adult and guardian_junior —
  because those are your two genuinely different conversations. Add streams as data justifies.

Two things this buys you. First, prompt iteration stops being a deploy: your growth person edits guardian_junior in the playground while engineering ships product. Second, the agent id is a first-class experiment variable — you can A/B two openers for the same stream and attribute conversion, because your backend already owns the outcome data.

The DPDPA line, drawn concretely

For the guardian persona, the context payload may carry the guardian's name, the stream, and the commercial facts. It must not carry the child's task scores, ability profile or progress — because passing child-derived behavioural data into a third-party targeting engine is the clearest possible instance of what §9(3) prohibits, and consent does not cure it. Enforce this as a whitelist on the outbound payload in your own code, not as a guideline for whoever writes the prompt.

Why "80% pickup on normal mobile numbers" should worry you, not attract you

High pickup on a regular 98/94 number means the traffic is not on the 140-series that TCCCPR requires for promotional calls — which is exactly the pattern TRAI's carrier-side ML profiling is built to detect, and exactly the pattern that gets a number blocked with no appeal. A vendor marketing this as a feature is marketing your regulatory exposure back to you. Treat "high pickup, normal number" as a compliance smell, not a performance metric.

7 · If you build anyway: the shortest reliable path

You asked for it, so here it is properly — and note that the shortest reliable path is not a greenfield build.

Do not build from parts. Adopt, then migrate.

The shortest reliable path is Bolna hosted → Bolna self-hosted, because it is the only path where the engine, the adapters and your integration code are identical on both sides. You learn the domain on someone else's operations, and the migration is a deployment exercise rather than a rewrite. Every other route re-solves problems Bolna's OSS core has already solved.

Phase 0 · nowBolna hosted PAYG. Build your side against their REST API. Days.
→
Phase 1 · month 2Own the telephony contract directly — BYO Plivo/Exotel SIP into Bolna. You now hold the numbers and DLT, they still run the engine. The blocker, de-risked in isolation.
→
Phase 2 · triggerAt ~25,000 min/mo, deploy the OSS engine on K8s alongside. Same adapters, same API shape.
→
Phase 3Shift traffic percentage-wise. Keep hosted as the failover. Rebuild only the playground you actually miss.

Phase 1 is the important one and you should do it regardless of whether Phase 2 ever happens. It separates the two risks you currently have bundled: "can we run an AI voice agent" and "can we own Indian telephony". Solve them one at a time, in that order, with the vendor absorbing whichever one you are not working on.
