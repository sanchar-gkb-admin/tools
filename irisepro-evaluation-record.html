Storyboard · 7 September 2026

The Evaluation Record

One screen that answers “what did this child submit, and why was it scored that way?” — built for the parent conversation, the Yashodarshi’s own review, and the admin resolving a dispute.

This exists because of a real escalation, not a hypothesis

On 7 September a parent challenged an evaluation. Answering it took direct SQL against production, S3 version archaeology, and three wrong turns — because there is no screen anywhere in YASHHO that shows a submission next to the scores it earned.

What we found could not have been found any other way: the child’s photographs attached to task 12 were her completed work for task 11 — the task that had been auto-scored zero for non-submission. She did the work, got nothing for it, and was then marked on it as though it were a different exercise.

01

Today — there is no surface

Not “a bad screen”. No screen. To answer one parent’s question we had to:

StepWhat it tookWhat went wrong
Find the studentSQL on studentsGuessed column name; it is full_name. Reported “no match” for a student who was there.
Find “task 12”Read units.task_snapshotTasks are shuffled per student. Her task 12 is task_id 11. task_id 12 is her position 16 — a lookup by ID answers the wrong question.
Judge the scoreParse score_snapshot JSONEvery task has a different maximum. Dividing by one task’s max produced “109.5%”.
See the workS3 head-objectRecordings 404’d. Nearly reported data loss — originals are deleted after review by design; optimised copies were there all along.

The point

Four traps, four near-miss wrong answers, on a question a parent is entitled to ask. Nobody without production access could have answered it at all — and the person who most needs the answer is the Yashodarshi being questioned.

Four decisions in that screen that change engineering, not styling

  1. Position is the identity, task_id is the footnote. Tasks are shuffled per student, so “task 12” only means anything as a position. Every surface — this screen, the scorecard, a support email — must lead with position and carry task_id only as a debugging aid. The parent, the Yashodarshi and the admin must all be counting the same thing.
  2. The maximum travels with the score, always. 133 is meaningless; 133 / 185 is not. Each task has its own maximum, already stored in score_snapshot. Never render a bare number.
  3. The media resolver is a first-class component. Video and audio originals are deleted at review by design; images are retained. The screen must silently serve the .optimized.* sibling and never show a broken tile — a 404 here reads as “we lost your child’s work”.
  4. Evidence must outlive the score it justifies. S3 expires task_responses/ at 90 days. A scorecard is discussed for months. Today this screen would 404 for any complaint older than a term.
⭐

Do not build this screen — it already exists

The Yashodarshi evaluation panel already renders everything above

resources/views/yashodarshi/task/evaluate-detail.blade.php — the screen an evaluator marks on — already shows, from its own header comment and markup:

  • The full brief — title, objective, description, instructions, deliverable, each when authored
  • The Specimen — the submission itself, with a full-frame lightbox: image, video, and PDF via the browser-native viewer
  • Every ability — mode-gated 0–5 dials snapping to 0.5, no hardcoded ability names
  • The feedback field, and the learning materials attached to the task

And CockpitPresenter::forSubmission() already re-loads the saved scores and feedback from YashodarshiEvaluationResult — its own comment reads “re-populate saved/draft feedback on re-open (was blank)”. Reopening a completed evaluation already shows the brief, the submission, and the marks that were given.

One line blocks both audiences

In YashodarshiAuthController::evaluateDetail():

if (!$submission->unit || $submission->unit->yashodarshi_id != $yashodarshi->id) { abort(403, 'Unauthorized access to this submission'); }

Together with the surrounding Auth::guard('yashodarshi'), that means: an admin cannot open the record at all — they are not a Yashodarshi, so they are redirected to the evaluator login — and a Yashodarshi loses access to their own past work the moment a unit is reassigned.

So the build is four small pieces, not a new screen

⛔ The evaluator's scope must key on who MARKED it, not on units.yashodarshi_id. Today's guard uses the latter — the unit's current assignment. Those two diverge the moment a unit is reassigned, and when they do an evaluator silently loses the ability to answer a question about work they did. It is also the wrong answer on its own terms: the person who can explain a score is the one who gave it, not whoever holds the unit today.

Why reuse beats rebuild here, beyond the saving

An audit must show what the evaluator actually saw. Any separately-built review screen drifts from the marking screen — a different image resolver, a different ability order, a newer rubric — and then a dispute turns into an argument about which screen is right. Rendering the same blade in read-only mode makes that class of argument impossible. It also means the four decisions above (position not task_id, the maximum travelling with the score, the media resolver, retention) are inherited rather than reimplemented.

03

The four cases it must survive

Taken from one real student’s 13 tasks. A design that handles only the happy path fails on her first week.

Case A · The work is attached to the wrong task

Position 11 closed at 12:11:24 and was auto-scored 0 / 165. Position 12 opened 11 seconds later. At 12:12:10 she uploaded two photographs — of her position 11 work — and they attached to position 12.

The screen must make this visible, not hide it. Show the previous task’s outcome inline whenever a submission lands within minutes of a prior expiry. An evaluator seeing previous task expired 11s earlier would have opened the images and stopped.

Case B · The task expired and nothing was submitted

A zero is a legitimate outcome, but the record must show why — window opened, window closed, nothing received — rather than a bare 0 that reads as “your child did badly”. This is the single most complaint-generating number we produce.

Case C · Scored with no feedback at all

12 of her 13 tasks carry no written feedback. Platform-wide only 27.6% of 10,578 evaluations do, and individual evaluators range from 0.3% to 99.8%. The record should state plainly when no feedback was given — silence should be visible, not inferred.

Case D · The media is not what the evaluator saw

The evaluator was served the optimised copy; the original may already be deleted. When an admin reopens a case months later they must be told which artefact they are looking at, so nobody argues about a file the evaluator never had.

Why show them their own feedback rate

He simply had no signal that written feedback was expected, because nothing ever told him 93% of his evaluations went out without any. A number he can see is worth more than a policy he was never shown. Pair it with the honest comparison, not a target.

The flag column is the whole design

An admin cannot read 21 tasks looking for something wrong. The screen must surface the anomaly itself: expired, submitted-suspiciously-soon-after-a-prior-expiry, scored-without-feedback, evaluated-in-under-30-seconds. On this student, two flags would have pointed straight at the problem — and this escalation would have been a five-minute conversation instead of a day.

06

Mobile — the parent call

A Yashodarshi or Chief takes the call on a phone. They need three things in the first screenful: what was submitted, what it scored, was there feedback. Ability detail collapses behind one tap; the submission opens full-screen. Nothing else belongs there.
