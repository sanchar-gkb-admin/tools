WhatsApp Flows,
not a chatbot

The console storyboard showed what we manage. This shows what Priya experiences — real chat bubbles, real in-chat forms, and where the branches are. The headline decision: use Meta's native Flows for every structured step, and keep an AI bot only as the fallback for free text.

60–90sto complete a Flow · vs 5–10 min in chat
12%abandonment · vs 35% question-by-question
+40–60%completion when lead capture moves to a Flow
1 Oct 2026service replies become billable — a Flow avoids it
₹0.145the only paid message in the whole booking journey
Why this matters beyond conversion. Free-form replies inside the 24-hour service window are free today — but from 1 October 2026 service replies sent by a human agent or a third-party AI agent inside that window become billable. A twenty-message bot conversation that costs ₹0 today becomes a cost line in seven weeks. A Flow is one interaction, so it doesn't. Flows are both the better experience and the cheaper one after October.

11 · Kannada, and knowing when to stopThe bot answers the routine question and escalates the sensitive one rather than improvising.

WHO HANDLES WHAT — and why the split matters

  FLOW      every structured step        booking · child details · objection
            deterministic · one interaction · clean data · cheapest

  BOT       free text only               FAQ · "is this right for my child?"
            fallback when someone types instead of tapping

  HUMAN     anything sensitive           learning needs · money worry · complaint
            the bot's job is to RECOGNISE these, not answer them

THE ESCALATION RULE — write it into the bot brief
  hand over on:  a child's health or learning difficulty
                 anything about money the Flow did not cover
                 any sign of distress
                 two failed attempts to answer the same question

  A bot that improvises on a learning-difficulty question does more damage
  than one that says "let me get a person" — this is a brand risk, not a UX nicety.

Three independent capability gaps now point the same way. The earlier BSP recommendation weighted AiSensy's published price and 10-minute activation heavily — reasonable when the requirement was broadcast plus a bot. Now that the design depends on Flows, that weighting looks wrong.

Send this today: "Do you support Meta's native WhatsApp Flows? Specifically (a) can we create and update Flows via API or only in the dashboard, and (b) do you support DYNAMIC Flows with an endpoint_uri so a Flow can fetch live data from our server mid-form — for example real session availability?"

If the answer is "static only" or "dashboard only", switch to Gupshup. The booking Flow is the centre of this design and a static one cannot show live seats.
