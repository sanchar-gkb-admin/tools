The n-gate on form 19 matters more than it looks. The equivalence rule was p_max − p_min ≤ 0.10 across a slot's forms. That is a range statistic, and a threshold on a range means nothing without n. The expected range of 22 perfectly equivalent forms under the null is d₂(22)·√(0.25/n) = 0.349 at n=30 · 0.270 at n=50 · 0.191 at n=100. So as written the rule quarantines healthy slots. The author pulls a good form, the pool drops below target_n, generation degrades, and nothing says so. No verdict may render before it can be true.

Waiting for n is necessary and not sufficient. Solving that expression for 0.10 gives n ≈ 365 responses per form — 8,030 per slot, 803,000 per paper, about 380 Advanced students per paper before the check that the design calls "the only real equivalence check" can speak at all. But choosing n so that the expected null range equals the threshold means, by definition, half of perfectly equivalent slots still exceed it. So the fix is not "wait longer" — it is a proper test: a two-proportion z of each form against its slot's pooled p excluding itself, with the family-wise correction stated (Bonferroni zcrit = 3.05 across one slot's 22 forms, 4.24 across a paper's 2,200). A02 must also say which checks are live and which are dormant, so nobody reads a quiet slot as a validated one.
Same cause, different symptom. A form added in month 6 is only ever drawn by students already ten attempts in — a more practised, self-selected population. Its p is inflated by cohort, not difficulty. So the rule's most reliable behaviour is to flag the newest content as too easy, which is exactly the content the 137-of-400 shortfall depends on. Store p_value_by_attempt_index[], not a scalar, and compare forms only at the same attempt index.

The tier ladder is gone, and it took a problem with it. target_n was a single scalar while the old tiers needed 4 / 8 / 22 — one slot cannot hold three values, and sizing separately meant Basic and Advanced students answered different item sets with incomparable percentages. Under the set model there is one number: 9 forms — 8 sets plus a spare. Every student draws from one pool. 1,800 forms across both papers instead of 4,400, a 59% cut, and one author can finish it in 3.6–6 months.

And the weights are not ours to write. KMM authors the ability mapping; this screen validates it. That is the right split — the judgement about what a question demands belongs to the framework's author, and the arithmetic about whether the resulting vector can support a claim belongs here.

And position must be randomised per student. Slot identity was fixed to (paper_id, seq_no) with shuffling only within an area — but both source papers are positionally blocked by area (GS-1's 16 areas fall in 22 contiguous runs; GS-2 is three blocks of 40/30/30). So position predicts area and area predicts ability, for every student, on every attempt, forever. By attempt 5 a student has the blueprint; one study group has the paper's full specification. Randomising position costs nothing now and is a migration later.

The friction is deliberate — requiring a named misconception per wrong option is what prevents fillers like "only mountain climbing". It was assumed this fought the length rule, since real distractors carry qualifying clauses and fillers are two words. We ran the exemplar pair. It doesn't.

Take a genuine higher-order item whose correct option needs a clause (129 chars) against lazy distractors (28, 40, 18): spread 7.17×, fails. Now rewrite those three as beliefs candidates actually hold — the 42nd Amendment origin story, the State-vs-Union confusion, the Article 31C inversion — and they come out at 84, 82, 73. Spread 1.77×, passes. Writing better distractors closes the gap rather than widening it, because a real misconception needs about as much qualification as a real answer.

But the correct option is still the longest of the four. The item satisfies the rule and fails the rule's purpose — which is the actual defect: max/min ≤ 2.0× constrains spread, and the tell lives in rank. Trim the correct option to 57 chars and it becomes the shortest of the four, spread 1.47×, still a higher-order item with three real misconceptions, tell gone.
So the rule changes. Length-ratio is replaced by rank uniformity: across the bank — and per ability, since the tell concentrates in Comprehension and Critical & Analytical — the correct option should be the longest about a quarter of the time, the shortest about a quarter, and so on. χ²(3) against uniform, critical value 7.815. This subsumes the old rule and closes its mirror exploit: a bank at 0% longest-correct passes a one-sided ceiling while making "never pick the longest" worth roughly 22 marks.

No per-form verdict — a single item's rank carries no information. A04 shows a running budget instead, and the whole discipline is one sentence an author can hold: roughly one question in four should have the shortest option correct.

A06 · Quarantine — NEW

Quarantine stops a form being drawn. It does not decide what happens to the answers already given to it — and that is a measurement decision, not housekeeping.

Cancel
Why this screen exists at all. The design said "quarantine ≠ retire: existing variants are untouched". That is right for reproducibility and wrong for measurement — a form quarantined for diverging has been declared not a member of its slot, yet its responses stay in every mean forever, and nothing on any card records it.
