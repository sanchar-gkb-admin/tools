Revision v2 — 14 Aug, after checking the APIs

This document originally recommended AiSensy outright. Two findings changed that, and one of them is a fault in my own process:

  • AiSensy's public API is a single endpoint. No template create/edit/list, no campaign creation — both are dashboard-only. For a team building its own orchestration that is a thin surface (§4b).
  • Gupshup exposes ~25+ documented endpoints, including full template management and blocked-user listing.
  • And Gupshup was never explicitly eliminated. It was in the 20-candidate long list, and it simply did not resurface in the finalists — a silent drop, which is precisely what a written funnel is supposed to make impossible. Corrected in §2.

The recommendation is now conditional on three answerable questions (§10). Nothing about the number strategy (§5) or the Kannada template maths (§6) changes.

1 · What changed, and what we are actually buying

Three decisions narrowed this from a platform purchase to a channel purchase — and that narrowing is what makes the choice easy.

DecisionConsequence for BSP selection
No AI calling — most expensive line item, not cost-effective at current scale Removes voice from the requirement set entirely. No BSP needs a dialler
We build, own and operate the nurture process — Geeta ji documents the flow; we build the CRM to orchestrate it The single most important consequence. We are NOT buying a journey builder, a CRM, a lead-scoring engine or a drip engine. We need a reliable pipe with good APIs. Most of what BSPs charge for is now redundant to us
Choose the best BSP for WhatsApp messaging Scope: broadcast · event-driven sends · inbound chatbot · Kannada · numbers

So the buy list is short, and the "don't buy" list is long

WE BUY — the pipe and the inbound brain

  • WhatsApp Business API access + number hosting
  • Broadcast engine with real throughput
  • Inbound chatbot builder — enrolment/registration + support FAQ. This is the one genuinely conversational surface, and it is cheaper to configure than to build
  • Template management incl. Kannada
  • Send/receive/status APIs and webhooks

WE DO NOT BUY — we own these

  • CRM / contact system of record
  • Lead scoring and auto-tagging
  • Journey / drip orchestration our scheduler, our journey state
  • Analytics and attribution
  • The customer timeline

This inverts the usual ranking. Every BSP comparison online ranks on inbox polish and journey-builder depth. For us those are near-worthless. We rank on API quality, broadcast throughput, bot capability and price per message.

The process fault, recorded so it is not repeated

Gupshup was in the 20-candidate list and no filter ever removed it. It was sorted into the "enterprise" bucket early and never re-examined — so it failed to appear in the finalists without a written reason. Two lessons:

  • A funnel only works if every elimination is explicit. An implicit drop is invisible, and invisible drops are how the right answer gets lost.
  • The filter order encoded a wrong assumption. I ranked platform fee above API depth. Once we decided to build the orchestration ourselves (§1), API depth became the primary axis — and the filter order should have been rewritten at that moment, not after.

3 · The five filters, and what each killed

3.1  The painful elimination: Ozonetel

Ozonetel has 621 G2 reviews at ~4.6 — by a wide margin the strongest usage evidence of any vendor in this category, and it is a Meta-certified BSP with omnichannel routing. It is eliminated purely by Filter 2: Asian pricing is quoted through sales only. On a cost-first brief with no revenue, a vendor that will not publish a price is a vendor that expects an annual commitment. If that premise changes — if they will do a published-price monthly plan — Ozonetel deserves to re-enter at the top of the list. Worth one email to find out.

5 · How many numbers — and the answer that changed

I previously advised splitting transactional and marketing numbers on day one. Further evidence says that was too strong, and on a cost-first brief it is the wrong first move.

The correction, stated plainly

I argued a marketing blast could tank your quality rating and take your OTP delivery with it. Two things soften that:

  • A Red rating primarily freezes your tier and blocks upgrades rather than cutting delivery outright (sources differ on whether Meta can also demote a tier — treat demotion as possible, not certain).
  • Utility and authentication are a separate category and keep flowing when marketing is restricted. Meta's US +1 case demonstrates this: marketing templates stopped delivering while utility and auth continued.

So the catastrophic scenario I implied is milder and slower than I said. Meanwhile the cost is concrete: ₹2,499/month per extra number on Wati, unpublished elsewhere.

5.1  The real argument for splitting is opt-out, not quality

Quality rating recovers. A block does not. Meta requires you to honour any request to block or opt out, and both are per number. So the failure that actually matters is: a guardian receives a marketing message they did not want, blocks the number — and because that number also carries OTP, login links and scorecards, you have permanently lost your delivery channel to a paying customer. That is a product failure, not a marketing one, and no tier recovers it.

The answer: ONE number now. Split at a named trigger, not on a hunch.

PHASE 1 — TODAY (~600 contacts)
  ONE number   utility + auth + marketing + inbound bot
              ₹0 extra · one conversation thread per guardian · simplest timeline
              safe because volume is low, lists are first-party, and every send is targeted

PHASE 2 — SPLIT WHEN ANY ONE OF THESE FIRES
  ▸ marketing sends exceed ~5,000/month
  ▸ quality rating hits Yellow for the first time
  ▸ we send to any list that is not first-party and freshly opted in
  ▸ block/opt-out rate exceeds 0.5% of a send
              → add a SECOND number for marketing only; number 1 becomes brand+transactional+bot

NEVER
  ▸ a third number. Two is the ceiling — beyond that the guardian cannot tell who we are

Why start with one: it is free, it keeps one thread per guardian (which matters because our unified timeline is being built around exactly that), and the split is additive later — you add a number, you do not migrate one. The reversible order is one-then-two, not two-then-consolidate.

5.2  Which number, and can it be toll-free?

6 · Kannada, and the hidden operational cost

Kannada is supported by everyone. The thing that will actually cost you is not support — it is template multiplication.

The fact that changes how you plan content

WhatsApp supports Kannada (with Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati). But: each language must be submitted as a SEPARATE template. A single bilingual template will not pass approval.

So the real cost of multilingual is N × every template, each needing its own Meta approval (24–48h standard; 1–2h for authentication), each needing a Kannada copywriter, and each needing version control when the copy changes.

Design consequence — put this in Geeta ji's process document: a template is a versioned content asset in N languages, not a message. Keep the canonical text in our own system with the language variants alongside, and treat the BSP as the place they are published, not authored. That way switching BSP later is a re-submission, not a rewrite — and it keeps the template library under our version control rather than in a vendor's dashboard.

Practical sequencing: launch English + Kannada only. Add Hindi when the first non-Karnataka cohort justifies it. Every language you add multiplies template ops forever, so add them on evidence, not ambition.

8 · Why this works short term AND long term

The honest version: the vendor choice is right for now and will probably be wrong later — and the architecture is what makes that acceptable.

Short term — why AiSensy is right today

  • Lowest entry cost ₹1,500, no annual commitment
  • 5 free agent seats — the others charge; at a 3-person team that alone is worth ₹2,250/mo
  • Fastest broadcast found (10k in ~18 min) — matters when a scorecard release or a batch announcement is time-bound
  • AI chatbot trained on business data — the enrolment + FAQ bot is configuration, not a build
  • 2 lakh contact import covers the "10 lakh" ambition in 5 passes
  • Publishes its numbers — rates, throughput, seats. You can hold them to it

Long term — why it stays safe even when it stops being cheapest

  • Because we own the orchestration, the BSP is a channel adapter. Contacts, journey state, lead score, consent and the timeline live in our Laravel backend. The BSP holds no state we cannot reproduce
  • Templates stay under our version control (§6) — switching becomes re-submission, not rewrite
  • The number is portable. A WhatsApp number can be migrated between BSPs; it is our asset, not theirs
  • So the exit cost is days, not quarters — and that is the real long-term protection. Not picking the vendor that will still be right in 2028, but making sure being wrong costs a week
  • The re-evaluation trigger is defined: at ~10,000 marketing messages/month, re-price against Interakt and Gupshup. At that point per-message markup is the whole game

The principle underneath both columns

Do not try to pick the vendor you will never have to leave. Pick the cheapest vendor that meets today's need, and spend the saved effort making sure leaving is cheap. That is the same reasoning that killed the AI-calling build and the CRM purchase: keep the state and the logic where they are ours, rent the commodity, and never let a vendor hold something we cannot reproduce. Applied consistently across all three decisions, it is also why they compose rather than conflict.
