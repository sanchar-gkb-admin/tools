Institutional billing · design of record · 23 November 2026
Design before build · R110 · contract §3.23

The school is invoiced.The money arrives in three.

The storyboard says it plainly: the ₹15,000 package is paid in three instalments, and the RSM raises each invoice and records each instalment when the money lands. That is a different animal from a family paying by card — there is no checkout, no Razorpay order, no single moment where "paid" is decided. This page is the design of that second money path: the records, the arithmetic, who records and who confirms, how the chasing and the reconciliation work — and the seven rulings of 23 Nov that fix its numbers.

What exists todayOne-shot gateway payments, offers with negotiated prices and seat caps, an attribution stamp naming the institution and both earners, and commission accrued per payment. Plus an institutions table from July that nobody bills against yet.
What this addsAgreements, a schedule of instalments per offer line, tax invoices, recorded collections (partial ones too), the weekly seat batch that a realised payment unlocks, commission per collection, aging, reminders, Finance confirmation and an institution statement. No school login, no school checkout, no second ledger.

Two money paths, one ledger

A school does not check out. It signs, gets invoiced, and pays by bank transfer or cheque. The design keeps that money entirely out of the gateway path, and still pays commission through the one ledger and the one calculator that already exist.

Gateway path — unchanged

A family or a person pays through Razorpay: payments, a checkout link, the webhook amount gate, the offer redemption. One payment, one seat, commission keyed payment:{rzp}:…. This remains the only path a storefront or a family link can use.

Invoice path — new

A school pays against a tax invoice, and an RSM records each instalment collected. Commission is keyed collection:{id}:…. The collection row is the money event; nothing downstream waits for a gateway callback that will never come.

A school never checks out, and a link never prices its line

An institutional line is not sold through /go/{slug} and never appears in a storefront catalogue. The negotiated price resolves only through the institution's offer when a referred family pays for its own scan; a batch's money — package, Acharya, Academy — always moves through the invoice path. Keeping the two apart is what stops a school payment being counted as a family sale, or a family sale being counted as an instalment.

The seven records, and nothing more

Each record answers one question and is frozen once it has to be. Money is integer paise everywhere. Rows are appended, not edited; a mistake is corrected by a credit note, never by rewriting history.

How it fits the catalogue ruling

The schedule hangs off an offer line (R109.3), so a single agreement can carry 1,000 package seats at one negotiated price, 200 Acharya seats at another and 60 Academy seats at a third — each with its own instalments and its own closing cap. Nothing here is a per-school SKU, and every price still comes from the one price authority through the institution's offer.

One instalment, end to end

The May instalment for a 640-student college, as KMM's RSM-19 describes it — followed through every record it touches.

Agreement
The school signs; the RSM approvesAn agreement is recorded against the institution, naming the YSP and the RSM. The offer line already exists with its negotiated price and seat cap. Nothing is owed yet and no commission moves.
Schedule
Three instalments are written640 students × ₹5,000 net, three times: May, August, November 2027. Each instalment invoices ₹5,900 per seat with 18% GST, and the schedule is validated to sum to the line's negotiated net total.
Invoice
The RSM raises the May invoice — ₹37,76,000 ₹32,00,000 net plus ₹5,76,000 GST, numbered in the year's series, with both GSTINs and the place of supply. The gross, the GST split and the net are frozen at that moment and rendered as a tax invoice.
Reminders
The system chases, once per stepFifteen days before the due date, on it, a week after and a month after. The coordinator gets the invoice by email from the YASHHO billing desk, and the RSM sends the WhatsApp nudge; the YSP is copied. One reminder per invoice per step — a rerun never sends twice.
Collection
The money lands; the RSM records itThe date, the amount, the mode and the UTR — plus any TDS the school withheld, which together with the bank credit is the amount settled. Recording it twice is refused. A short payment is recorded as it is; the remainder stays outstanding.
Batch
The next Sunday batch starts — the seats that were paidSettled money ÷ ₹5,900 per seat gives the seats this collection unlocked; they join the next weekly batch, typically 30–50 seats. Each unlocked seat's scan starts. The roster is only the candidate list — the realised payment is what starts a seat.
Commission
Both earners are paid on the net collected The designated YSP at 5% and the designated RSM at 0.5% — the same institutional rate on every line (package, Acharya, Academy) — on the collection's net, TDS included, written once to the one ledger.
Confirm
Finance matches it to the bankA recorded collection is confirmed against the bank credit (plus any TDS certificate). Until then the field sees "recorded", Finance sees "recorded, not confirmed", and the unmatched view lists it.
Statement
The school's page shows the balance Invoices, collections and what is still due, computed from the rows — never from a stored total. August and November repeat the same walk on the same seats.

The arithmetic, ruled

Commission is a share of net revenue — what is left after GST — never of what the school sent. The invoice freezes both numbers so no later change can silently move a payout.

One instalment, worked through

The negotiated price is net, and the school pays 18% GST on top (ruled 23 Nov 2026). The 640-seat May instalment is therefore ₹32,00,000 net + ₹5,76,000 GST = ₹37,76,000 invoiced. On the net, the YSP earns 5% = ₹1,60,000 and the RSM 0.5% = ₹16,000.

If the school pays in two bank credits, each collection carries its gross de-GST'd at the frozen rate, and the final collection absorbs the paise residual so the collections sum exactly to the invoice net. Commission runs once per collection on that net — and on any TDS withheld, because the school settled it.

One source figure is superseded. RSM-19's unit test treated ₹32,00,000 as gross and got ₹1,35,593.22; that assumed an inclusive fee. The 23 Nov ruling rejects it: the same seats now invoice ₹37,76,000 and the net, and both commission lines, are exact in the lakhs.

Credit notes, not edits

Overpayment is refused. A genuine correction — our own error, a seat removed before the term starts — is a credit note that debits both earners pro rata to the credited net. The original ledger rows stay visible forever. A school that withholds, disputes or exits mid-term is not credited: that is a commercial conversation and the published no-refund policy stands. A credit note is a correction, never a refund product.

Seats, payments and batches

A seat does not start because a list arrived. It starts because money did.

A realised first instalment unlocks and starts a seat

Invoiced ₹5,900 per seat for the first instalment (₹5,000 net + GST). Settled money ÷ that per-seat price, floored, is the number of seats a collection unlocks; the remainder carries to the next collection. Nothing else gates a seat — the roster is the candidate list, uploaded when the agreement is approved, and a student's first task is still what makes it a sale (R107.1), not what starts it.

Paid seats start in a weekly batch

Seats are delivered in batches on a configured cadence — Sunday by default. A batch takes the seats whose first instalment was realised by its cut-off: typically 30–50, up to 1,000, whatever the payments support, never above the line's cap (R109.3). Unpaid seats wait for a later batch; a batch is never padded with money that has not arrived.

The combo unlocks in two steps

The annual package is a scan plus monthly Godparent, so its components are instalment-gated: the scan starts on instalment 1, the Godparent on instalment 2 — never before its gate is paid and never earlier than the month-2 offset (R109.2). Instalments 2 and 3 bill the same seats again and add none; they fund the package year, and instalment 3 unlocks no new component. The window is fixed — a late payment never moves the seat's start or the 12-month anniversary; it shortens the months delivered, and Godparent runs month-on-month for as long as the money behind it is realised. Late money has a path, not an instant cut: a 30–60 day grace window keeps Godparent running with no interruption; unpaid at its end the package pauses (the scan already delivered stands); unpaid 120 days past the due date the line is terminated as not paid, and what was collected is not refunded. A package whose second instalment never comes runs the scan and nothing else; it never silently degrades to scan-only.

The deal's active status for reporting still moves on the first collection against the first instalment. Everything else — aging, reminders, the unmatched-credit view — follows the money, not the batch.

Who records, who confirms

Recording money and having money reconciled are two different acts, and the design refuses to blur them — that blur is how a field report and a bank statement stop agreeing.

Chasing and reconciling — designed in, not bolted on

The follow-ups are the reason this is a project rather than a field. Each is a small, idempotent piece with a visible list, so nothing depends on somebody remembering.

Aging, derived

Per invoice: outstanding = gross − collected, bucketed 0–30, 31–60, 61–90 and 90+ days past due, plus "not yet due". Computed from the rows on every read; never a stored number that can rot.

Reminders, once per step

Offsets and channels are a dated config version. The invoice goes by email from the YASHHO billing desk; the nudge goes by WhatsApp from the designated RSM, with the YSP copied. A daily command writes one row per invoice per offset before it sends, so a rerun or a second box never double-sends. Logs carry invoice numbers and counts — never a full phone number or email.

Reconciliation

Two lists: collections recorded but not yet confirmed, and bank credits that match no invoice reference. Both visible; neither silently zero. This is where "the school says it paid" becomes a fact Finance can stand behind.

The school's statement

Invoices, collections and balance on the institution's page, rendered from the same rows. A coordinator's call ("what do we owe?") gets one answer, and it is the one Finance sees.

Two totals that never disagree

Commission is computed from collections, not from the invoice. A ₹37,76,000 invoice with ₹18,88,000 collected earns on the ₹16,00,000 net those collections carry, and the invoice stays part-paid. This is why the schedule, the invoice and the collection are three records and not one: collapsing them is how "invoiced" becomes "paid" by accident.

What stays frozen

One ledger and one commission calculator, used by both paths. One price authority; a school's price still resolves only through its offer. No school login and no school payee. No institutional family, no school-owned family. No gateway checkout for a school. Rates, reminder offsets and schedule shapes are dated config versions with a proposer and an approver; nothing about a particular school is hard-coded. Append-only money: corrections are credit notes, and a recorded collection is never edited.

The rulings — fixed 23 Nov 2026

None of these is open any more. Each one is recorded in the contract (R110.13) and binds the build; items (i)–(v) unblock slice A, and (vi)–(viii) the chasing, pause/termination and correction parts of slice B.

#QuestionRuling
(i)Are ₹15,000 / ₹5,000 per instalment / ₹1,00,000 GST-inclusive, and is the service taxable?Exclusive at 18%. The negotiated price is net; the school pays GST on top (₹15,000 → ₹17,700 invoiced). The offer item stores the gross, as R105.6(a) already requires. Taxable, as the direct scan already is. **Every figure in KMM's document is net revenue**; leadership's GST-inclusive billings view is display only and never the target denominator.
(ii)Commission rounded per invoice total or per student?Per invoice, and the final collection absorbs the paise residual so the collections sum exactly to the invoice net.
(iii)Tax point: GST due at issue or at receipt?Earlier of the two. An invoice issued in May and paid in June recognises GST in May; a prepayment before invoicing recognises it on the advance. Commission stays per collection.
(iv)What unlocks and starts a seat, and when?A realised first instalment. A seat starts when its first instalment is realised, in the next weekly batch (Sunday by default; 30–50 typical, up to 1,000 — the size is what was paid). The roster is only the candidate list. Because the package is a combo, its scan unlocks on instalment 1 and its Godparent on instalment 2; instalments 2–3 add no seats.
(v)Does a school deduct TDS?Recorded as a settlement line. Bank credit + TDS = amount settled; the invoice settles at gross; commission is on the full net settled, TDS included.
(vi)Reminder channels and senderEmail from the YASHHO billing desk (with the invoice) and WhatsApp from the designated RSM, the YSP copied.
(vii)What may a credit note cover?Correction of our own error only. A withholding, dispute or mid-term exit is not credited; the no-refund policy stands.
(viii)What happens when the second instalment is late?Grace, then pause, then termination. A 30–60 day grace window keeps Godparent running with no disruption; unpaid at its end the package pauses (Godparent stops, the scan stands); unpaid 120 days past the due date the line is terminated as not paid. The anniversary never moves, and money already collected is not refunded.

The three slices

Each slice is its own PR and its own deploy, with the new tables read only by new code. Nothing ships to a real school until slice A is green with the rulings above implemented.

The tests that matter most

The schedule sums to net × seats × (1 + GST), always. Invoice numbers are gap-free under concurrency. A frozen split ignores a later rate change. The same (invoice, reference) cannot be recorded twice. A partial payment earns on the amount received, and the collections together earn exactly rate × net. A credit note reverses both lines and leaves the originals. A seat starts only on a realised first instalment, in the next batch; instalments 2–3 add no seat; Godparent never starts before instalment 2. A late instalment keeps Godparent running through its grace window, pauses it at the window's end and terminates the line at 120 days past due. A YSP recording is refused. No institutional line ever reaches the gateway or the storefront. And no log line ever carries a full phone number or email.
