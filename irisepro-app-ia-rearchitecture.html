Design proposal · 26 August 2026

One scan, many vehicles
an IA re-architecture

The learner app was built to deliver one thing: a scan. It now has to deliver papers, sets, combos and boosters — each with its own unit of work, its own clock and its own idea of "done". This is where they live, and how onboarding bends to fit.

Today. mobile.dashboard — 2,686 lines of Blade. It is not a dashboard; it is the task list of the one scan you bought.

The constraint, stated exactly.

There is no layer between "the app" and "the tasks of my one scan." Home is the scan. So a second delivery vehicle has nowhere to go — it can only be jammed into the same list, or bolted on as a fifth tab, and both are wrong for the same reason: they treat a different kind of thing as another row of the same kind.

Everything downstream inherits the assumption. One active unit. One progress bar. One notion of "next". The task card, the timer, the media recorder and the submit flow are all shaped around record something, a human marks it, wait.

None of that is wrong. It is simply one vehicle's shape, sitting where the app's shape should be.

2 · What has to fit now

Four delivery vehicles, and they disagree about the most basic questions: what a unit of work is, who marks it, and what a clock means.

VehicleUnit of workWho scoresThe clock"Done" means
Scan · 1A–1Ca task — recorded answera YASHODARSHI, 0–5 on a 0.5 gridlearner-paced; per-task SLAall tasks submitted & evaluated
Exam hall · 2Aa paper (100 Q) + pre/post tasksmachine for the paper, human for the tasksa hard wall clock — it ends whether you do or notthe paper closed; both papers sat
Test series · 2Ba set = Paper 1 + Paper 2machinea fixed monthly calendarthe month's sets attempted
Combo · 3A/3Ba sequence of the abovecomposedeach leg keeps its ownevery leg complete, in order
The one thing they must NOT disagree about. Every vehicle resolves to one canonical scorecard for the learner. That is already the locked north star for MCQ — all paper variants yield one scorecard — and it is what stops the app fragmenting into four products that happen to share a login. Progress is about the learner. Delivery is about the purchase. Keep them on different axes and the architecture holds.

Do it. A task screen, or a paper screen. Deliberately different — one is paced, one is invigilated.

The rule that keeps the new layer from costing anything. A learner with exactly one active entitlement never sees the shelf — Home forwards straight into the vehicle, and their app is identical to today's, tap for tap. The shelf appears the moment there are two. The layer is charged only to the learners who need it.

One scorecard, many sources. "Where the evidence came from" is the join that lets four vehicles stay four vehicles without the learner having four selves.

Why this screen decides the architecture. If Progress is per-product, the app is a folder of four products and every cross-sell is a new login-shaped problem. If Progress is per-learner, the products become ways of gathering evidence about one person — which is the actual proposition, and the reason a combo is worth more than its legs.

"Not enough yet" is a feature. S12 already establishes the padlock for an ability with insufficient evidence. Reuse it verbatim rather than showing a number the evidence cannot carry.
Free consequence. With Progress global, mobile.scorecard and mobile.performance stop being two answers to one question and become one screen at two zooms — which is what the scorecard-at-three-zooms storyboard already specifies.

4 · Onboarding — it already adapts, on the wrong axis

This is the part worth being precise about, because the machinery exists and is good. OnboardingFamily shapes onboarding by audience — student, exam, aspirant, transition — each with its own profile fields, field options and study-guide variant, keyed off program_key.

What it does not vary by is the delivery vehicle. The one step that is shape-dependent — the pace chooser — resolves off tier: 
$tier = $this->challenge?->tier;
if ($tier === null) { return []; }          // ← no modes

$group = config('enrolment.mode_tier_groups')[$tier] ?? null;
if ($group === null) { return []; }         // ← no modes
mode_tier_groups knows 999 · 1999 · 4999 · 5999. Exam hall carries tier = NULL, so a 2A learner gets zero paces and cannot complete onboarding at all.

Two axes, not one. Onboarding needs both, and they are genuinely independent — a KAS aspirant buying a scan and a KAS aspirant buying a mock are the same person meeting two different instruments.

The seam already exists; point it at the right column. OnboardingFamily::shapeForChallenge() is the right shape of solution — a declared step-shape resolved from the product. Add the second resolver beside it, keyed on the product's kind (scan · mcq · exam_hall · combo), and delete the tier lookup. Consistent with the catalogue's north star: bundling a new product must be an INSERT, not a code change.

Onboard just-in-time. Setting up all three now means three pace/rules screens the learner will not remember by the time they matter — and a date chosen for a mock three months out.

The sequencing rule. A combo is a recipe, not a basket — its legs are ordered and the order carries meaning (measure → practise → rehearse). So: show all, onboard one, open the next when its predecessor closes. The shelf already renders "Coming up", so a queued leg needs no new surface.
One thing this does not settle. Whether a learner may re-order or run legs in parallel. Doing it in the bought order is the safe default and what the pricing assumes; anything else is a product decision, not an implementation detail.

5 · What has to change in code

Three defects, all the same root: tier standing in for product shape. Each one blocks exam hall independently, and each is cheap now and expensive after the first sale.

AllocationLoad::perTaskMinutes(string $tier)tierTypeError. And because the candidate sweep costs every live unit, one assigned exam-hall unit breaks allocation for all products

One fix, three defects. Derive from the product's kind, not its price. The catalogue already carries stream + plan for all 59 products; identity is (stream, plan) and tier is retired everywhere else. These three are the stragglers.

6 · Decisions this needs

#QuestionWhy it changes the build
D1Does the footer become Home · Progress · Standing · Me, or stay as it is with Home simply re-pointed?Merging Leaderboard + Achievements frees the slot Profile and Support need. Cosmetic on its own; it decides how many screens move.
D2Confirm Progress is per learner, never per product.The load-bearing decision. Everything else follows from it, and reversing it later means re-cutting every screen in §4.
D3Combo legs: strictly sequential, or may a learner start leg 2 early?Sequential is assumed above and is what the pricing implies. Parallel needs a different shelf and a different scorecard join.
D4Is the exam-hall sitting date learner-chosen, or fixed by cohort?Decides whether onboarding step 4 is a date picker or a confirmation — and whether YASHODARSHI capacity can be planned at all.
D5For a learner with one product, does Home forward straight into the vehicle?Recommended yes. It is what keeps this re-arch free for the majority of today's learners.
