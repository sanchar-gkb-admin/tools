Side by side

The same journey, both ways

Today — 7 steps

  1. Read the agreement on screen
  2. Download the PDF
  3. Print it
  4. Sign by hand
  5. Scan or photograph it
  6. Upload the scan + both sides of Aadhaar
  7. Wait up to 48 hours for staff approval

Signed before anyone knows who they are — in whatever name they typed.

Proposed — 4 steps

  1. Verify PAN — we learn their legal name
  2. Verify identity through DigiLocker — we learn their address
  3. Verify bank — ₹1 confirms the account is theirs
  4. Sign on screen, in the name on the PAN

Signed as a verified person, in a name checked against a government record. Nothing printed, nothing uploaded, nothing kept that could be misused.

Why the order matters The name on an agreement should be the name on the PAN card, exactly — not whatever a person types. Typed names vary: initials, spellings, married names, transliterations. An agreement signed as "R. Kumar" against a PAN reading "RAVI KUMAR" invites an argument we would rather not have. We cannot bind someone to their verified name before we know it.
And when a name legitimately differs K M Manjunath, Manjunath K M, Manjunath — all the same person. Rather than guess, the screen shows the name the bank returned and asks "Is that you?" One tap. The person is the authority on their own name.
