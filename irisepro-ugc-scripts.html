UGC Ad Scripts

THESE ARE PROMPTS, NOT LINES TO RECITE. Give the speaker the hook and the beats, then let them say it their own way. The moment it sounds performed, it stops being UGC and becomes an ad — which is the one thing this format cannot survive.

Shoot vertical, on a phone, in the room it actually happened in. No lighting, no tripod, no clean audio. Imperfection is the format's credential.

First two seconds decide everything. Every hook below starts mid-thought, because a sentence already in progress is harder to scroll past than one that begins politely.

Nobody promises a result. Speakers describe only what they personally saw. That is both the compliance rule and, as it happens, what makes UGC believable.

01 · Mother — the one to shoot first

"I wasn't going to post this"Mother · 35–45 · 40 sec
Hook · 0–2 sec

"I wasn't going to make this video, honestly. But I keep thinking someone else needs to hear it."

BEAT 1 — "My daughter used to study till midnight. Every night. And her marks never moved. Not once in two years."

BEAT 2 — "And I'll be honest with you — there was a point where I started thinking maybe something is wrong with her. And then I felt terrible for thinking that. What kind of mother thinks that."

BEAT 3 — "So we did this scan thing. Ninety minutes, at home, she didn't even have to prepare."

BEAT 4 — "And the report came and… she's not weak at anything. Her problem was planning. That's it. Planning. Nobody in ten years of school ever told me that."

CLOSE — "I'm not saying it fixed everything. I'm saying I stopped guessing. That's all I wanted."

Shot: Sitting on a bed or sofa, phone propped, slightly too close. Evening light. Tone: Confiding, not presenting — like she's telling one friend. Critical: Beat 2 must not be smoothed over. The guilt is the reason anyone watches to the end.

02 · Father — the shorter, harder cut

"Nobody could tell me why"Father · 40–50 · 25 sec
Hook · 0–2 sec

"Twelve years of school fees, and not one person could tell me why my son wasn't doing well."

BEAT 1 — "Teachers said he's not focused. Tuition said he needs more practice. Everyone had an opinion. Nobody had an answer."

BEAT 2 — "This was the first time somebody actually measured something and showed me a page. Not an opinion — a page."

CLOSE — "Whether you buy it or not, I don't care. Just stop guessing about your child. That's my only advice."

Shot: In a car, or standing outside — somewhere he'd naturally take a call. Tone: Slightly blunt, a little impatient. Why it works: "Whether you buy it or not, I don't care" is the least salesy line possible, which is precisely why it sells.

03 · Student — the one that will travel furthest

"I used to not talk"Student · 14–16 · 30 sec
Hook · 0–2 sec

"Two years back I wouldn't have been able to make this video. Like, at all."

BEAT 1 — "I used to not talk in class. Even if I knew the answer. Especially if I knew the answer, actually."

BEAT 2 — "I did this thing where they give you tasks. Not a test — tasks. One of them, I had to interview a shopkeeper. I nearly didn't go."

BEAT 3 — "And after that it just… got easier? I don't know how to explain it properly."

CLOSE — "My marks went up also. But that's not the thing I'd tell you about."

Shot: Her room, phone in hand, walking or sitting cross-legged. Let her be awkward. Do not script the close — ask her "what's the thing you'd actually tell someone?" and use whatever she says. Why it works: "That's not the thing I'd tell you about" makes marks incidental, which is the whole positioning, said by a teenager instead of a brand.

04 · KAS aspirant — third attempt

"The list came out"Aspirant · 24–30 · 35 sec
Hook · 0–2 sec

"Third attempt. Walked out of that hall knowing I knew it. Name wasn't on the list. Again."

BEAT 1 — "And you know what's worse than failing? Nobody can tell you what went wrong. So you just… start again. Same books. Same routine. Praying it's different."

BEAT 2 — "This year I did the scan before I opened the syllabus. First time I've done anything in that order."

BEAT 3 — "Turns out I was losing marks on questions I actually knew. Question selection. Timing. Not knowledge — I had the knowledge."

CLOSE — "I don't know how this attempt will go. But for the first time I know what I'm fixing."

Shot: At a study table, late, books visible. Tired, not defeated. Critical: the close must stay uncertain — "I don't know how this attempt will go" is what makes the whole thing credible, and it is also the only honest thing he can say.

05 · Founder — unpolished, phone, no set

"Someone asked me why I built this"Founder · 45 sec
Hook · 0–2 sec

"Someone asked me yesterday why a KAS officer would build an assessment company. Fair question."

BEAT 1 — "Because I sat in those exam halls. And I watched people who were far brighter than me not clear it. And people nobody expected, clear it."

BEAT 2 — "For years I thought it was luck. It wasn't luck. It was abilities that nobody was measuring — not the schools, not the coaching centres, nobody."

BEAT 3 — "So we built the thing that measures them. Sixty-four of them."

CLOSE — "Fifteen thousand students later, the thing parents write to us about most isn't marks. It's confidence. I did not expect that."

Shot: Phone held at arm's length, office or car, no title card until the end. Tone: Thinking aloud, not delivering. Why the close works: "I did not expect that" is the most persuasive sentence in the script — a founder admitting his own product surprised him cannot be read as a boast.

06 · The street test — not a script, a setup

"Name five things your child is good at"Vox pop · 30 sec
Hook · 0–2 sec

On screen, no voice: "We asked parents to name five things their child is good at."

THE SETUP — Stop real parents. One question, asked warmly. Then say nothing and keep rolling.

WHAT YOU WILL GET — Most manage two. Then a pause. Then "he's good at… studies?" Then a laugh that isn't quite a laugh.

THE CUT — Six or seven parents, back to back, each stalling at the same place. No commentary. No music.

END CARD — "There are 64. School reports on four." — then the logo.

Why this is the one I'd bet on: it makes no claim whatsoever, it cannot be accused of manipulating anyone, and it is the only idea here that people will send to each other. Ethics: get signed consent on the spot, and if a parent names five, use that clip too — the honesty is the point, and it will still land.

Hooks — test these before anything else

"I wasn't going to make this video, honestly."

"Twelve years of school fees and nobody could tell me why."

"Two years back I wouldn't have been able to make this video."

"Third attempt. Name wasn't on the list. Again."

"We asked parents to name five things their child is good at."

Run all five as 6-second cuts against the same audience. The winner earns the full film; the rest are answered questions, not wasted work.
