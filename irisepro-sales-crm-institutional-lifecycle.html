A plain guide for the field, the office and the board

A school is the customer.One deal, one ladder, many students.

Families and institutions are sold to in two different ways, and this guide says exactly how. It follows one school from the first visit to the last seat of its agreed batch, and it lines the institutional lifecycle up against the family one, so that anyone can see what is the same, what is genuinely different, and — most importantly — which parts are single pieces of machinery used twice rather than two systems that will drift. It is binding where it cites the rulings R105–R108; everything else here is explanation.

Family directA household buys for its own child. The customer is a family, the contact is a parent, and the child's data lives under that family's consent.
InstitutionalA school buys for its people. The customer is the school, the deal has stages of its own, and the students (or teachers) arrive as a roster on the school's link.

Two funnels, one owner

The single most important sentence in this document: every lead and contact belongs to exactly one funnel, and that is decided when the record is created — never re-decided later by looking at who paid for what.

The funnel is carried by the record

A family lead lives in the family funnel: a household record (crm_families) with its own consent, its own do-not-contact, and its children's data. An institution account lives in the institutional funnel: a school record whose people are adults buying for the school. The funnel is a property of the owning record and is inherited by everything that points at it — activities, links, stamps, reports. It is not a flag on each row that some later job could flip.

The old institutions table that tags enrolment records is not the CRM account, and it does not become one (R105.1). The institutional account is its own record, defined with the pipeline that reads it.

The invariant that decides every edge case

An institution's contact never enters crm_families (R105.2). There is no borrowed consent, no household do-not-contact and no children's-data semantics for a school. And the reverse holds too: a family that bought because a school pointed at Yashho is still a family — it stays in the family funnel and obeys every family rule. The institution is a tag on that sale, never the owner of the household.

What that buys us

One contact can never become two customers. A parent's number is found by the same identity map whether the lead came from a field visit, a joining list, a school roster or a payment (R108.11). A sale is credited in one funnel and counted once (R107.4). And privacy stays honest: the school's record holds adults, while any child's data stays inside the tenancy, under the family's own consent (C7, R56.6, R56.7).

The abstractions, and what each one is not

If this section is right, the rest of the build follows. Each row is one concept with one meaning, used by both funnels where that is honest and kept separate where the two genuinely differ.

The test we will keep applying

Whenever the institutional work needs behaviour that already exists for families, the question is not "how do we build it for institutions?" but "which one piece of machinery does this become a second use of?" A proposal that adds a parallel engine — a second importer, a second stage engine, a second commission formula, a second identity map — fails review by default.

Who is who, and who earns

A school relationship has three interests: the person who found it, the person who owns it, and the office that can price it. The design keeps all three visible at once, on the link itself.

Field · sourcing

The designated YSP

Finds and signs the school, and keeps the sale credit.

  • Named on the institution's link as its designated YSP.
  • Earns the own-sale commission rule on every participant payment through that link (R105.5).
  • May upload the school's roster; the claim runs as this post whoever uploaded (R108.3).
  • Sees the institution's calls, visits and students started in their own scope.
Field · relationship

The designated RSM

Owns the institution record and the relationship, and earns the network line.

  • Named on the link as its designated RSM.
  • Earns the RSM network rule (0.5%) on the same payments (R105.5) — no threshold_sales gate on this line.
  • Moves the deal with the YSP; approves the agreement per D17.
  • May upload the school's roster; the resulting families are still the designated YSP's (R108.3).
Head Office

The HO desk

Mints the institution link and keeps the negotiated offer honest.

  • Only a holder of campaign_link.create_hq creates an institution link — a field role never mints one (R105: the negotiated price and seat count are an HO line).
  • Chooses the governed destination and the UTMs; the code is minted, never chosen.
  • Sees both pipelines and can drill into an institution's deal.
Governance

GM and CEO

Own the configuration, not the deals.

  • The institutional pipeline is a governed value_list change: the GM proposes, the CEO approves, YSPs and RSMs never (R106.1).
  • Targets gain a funnel dimension, each a complete governed snapshot (R107.3).
  • Pricing stays with the CEO (D17); region- and institution-specific offers follow the same approval path as other assets (D59).
Ownership and credit are two different things — deliberately

D51 gives the RSM the institution record and the relationship; the YSP who sourced and signed it keeps the sourcing credit. R105.3 puts both people on the link, so a payment never has to guess who earned. Ownership decides who works the deal; designation decides who is paid. A family's ownership is untouched by any of this (D22, R26).

The school ladder — institutional, not a household's journey

A family's ladder follows a household from a new lead to an onboarded child. A school's ladder is a B2B closing sequence: visit the site, propose a price per seat, negotiate, sign, activate, sell seats, renew. They are two vocabularies, configured separately (R106).

Prospect prospect

A school exists and someone is working it. The account starts here — or at onboarding when an agreement is signed before the record is made (R106.7).

Contacted contacted

Someone at the school has been reached. The call is an institutional activity — about the school, never about a household (R106.6).

Visit needed needs_visit

A visit to the principal or the site is the qualification. Until the visit happens the deal stays here; this is the institutional equivalent of meeting the decision-maker.

Proposal proposal

A price per seat and a seat count are put to the school. One offer, one price for the whole batch, stored GST-inclusive (R105.6(a)).

Negotiation negotiation

Terms move. What can change is the offer (price and seats) as a new governed value — never a per-parent price at checkout.

Agreement agreement

The school signs or accepts. The RSM approves the agreement (D17). Nothing is sold yet, and no commission moves.

Onboarding onboarding

The two designated posts are named and the negotiated offer is activated — in one in-app flow (R106.7). This is the only way a school enters the funnel.

Active active

Seats are selling on the school's link. Not terminal: participants keep paying and starting. Moved automatically by the first paid redemption through an institutional link (R106.5).

Renewal due renewal_due

The last seat of the agreed batch is redeemed. Moved automatically, and the link closes with "offer ended" — it never falls back to the list price. The school agrees a new batch to continue (R105.6(a), R106.5).

Outcomes, not stages

Deferred (still possible later), lost (terminal) and no consent (terminal, and only from the institution's own do-not-contact). As on the family ladder, a manual move must come from a listed from-stage and carry the ability; an automatic move may only name an event the system actually emits, so a rule can never point at a listener nobody has (R106.2, R106.4).

Same engine, separate vocabulary

Both ladders are rows in the same version-scoped configuration tables (cfg_stages, cfg_stage_transitions), validated by the same schema, applied by the same crm:config:apply and rolled the same way (R31, R43, R47.2). What differs is the content: the institutional ladder is its own config family, institution_pipeline, under config/sales-crm/institution_pipeline/. A version is a complete snapshot — nothing is edited in place. Adding a stage, a reason or an outcome is a dated config version, never a release (R76, R107.5).

The family ladder today reads: new → profiled → assigned → contact_pending → contacted → need_identified → interested → payment_pending → paid → handed_over → onboarded, with nurture, deferred, unqualified, lost, no_consent, unable_to_contact as outcomes. Nothing on this list moves a school.

Open point, flagged rather than papered over

R106.1 says no stage key may be shared between the two ladders; R106.2's seed names four institutional outcomes with the same words the family ladder uses (contacted, deferred, lost, no_consent). R106.9's own test ("no family stage key appears in the institutional version") would fail on that seed. The resolution is a config-version decision — most simply an inst_ prefix on every institutional key — and needs no code. It is recorded here so the seed is written correctly the first time.

What a school buys — three products, one catalogue

A school does not buy "an institutional programme". It buys one or more of three products, each a row in the same sellable-products catalogue the family scans live in, each priced by that school's own offer line and each capped by its own seats (R109). Every one of them is a batch: the school buys the seats, and the people who receive them never checkout.

The offer — a line per product, seats per line

One school, one offer, a line for each product it buys: 1,000 package seats, 200 Acharya seats and 60 Academy seats can sit on the same agreement, each at its own negotiated GST-inclusive price (R109.3).

Neither the price nor the seats come from the catalogue: the catalogue says what may be sold, the offer says at what price and how many. No per-school SKU is ever minted (R109.1).

The seats are bought by the school. A participant receives a seat the school paid for; the participant does not buy one (R109.4, R109.5). That is why an institutional line's commission accrues on the money collected against the offer line, not on a checkout — and why "how many seats an instalment covers" is part of the invoice question below.

Mission Acharya — the scan product for teachers, listed under the academic products catalogue

Acharya is a teacher-strengthening programme for schools, and it is an institutional offering (R108.13). It uses the same scan instrument and engine; what differs is the audience — audience = teacher — and the landing: its participants are the school's people, adults, never crm_families (R105.2). It is listed under the academic products catalogue, at ₹4,999 net per teacher (KMM-confirmed; ₹5,898.82 invoiced). It is a separate product with nothing to do with the annual package — the package's scan and Godparent mentoring are students-only — and it carries no Godparent. It is sold as a batch, not seat by seat (Goutam, 23 Nov): the school buys the seats, and its teachers are the batch's delivery — a roster, never individual checkouts. The catalogue lists the platform's existing acharya_* product by platform_product_key; nothing about it is free, demo or hidden.

The Academy is offline — and parked for now

The six Academy courses are annual, delivered offline through the partner institution (Goutam, 23 Nov: "the offline product will be delivered offline anyways as its more like a course delivered through our partner institutions"). Seats are sold to the school as a cohort batch, never as individual admissions — "not … one a piece". The CRM records the sale, the seats, the targets and the commission; it creates no digital entitlement, batch or login. The academic year is the offer's start and end, and a course seat is never converted into a scan or a package seat (R109.5).

Open points, named rather than guessed

(a) The GST reading is ruled (23 Nov 2026): ₹15,000 and ₹1,00,000 are net and GST is added at 18%, so a package seat invoices ₹17,700 and the offer line stores that gross; RSM-19's inclusive unit test is superseded. (b) No invoice or instalment entity exists yet — a batch cannot be invoiced, and its commission cannot run, until one is built; the design is institution billing (R110), and its seven rulings are recorded. (c) Godparent's own design is a draft board (PR #1904), and 1 Jan 2027 is the gate. (d) The Academy's collection path is ours (our invoice, our commission); a partner that collects is a new ruling, not a setting. (e) KMM's batch money path is designed: the RSM raises an invoice per instalment and records each instalment when collected (D17; part2_rsm.txt RSM-19 — May / Aug / Nov 2027, idempotent on invoice + payment reference, partial payment assumed). None of it exists yet — no institution, agreement, invoice, instalment or collection row, no aging or collection reminder, no reconciliation. The full design, with GST, rounding, tax point, seat start, TDS, reminders and credit-note grounds all ruled on 23 Nov, is institution billing (R110), in three build slices. (f) The Academy is parked for now (D70): designed and catalogued, not sold until a partner per Region and the course list are committed.

The institutional rate is one rate for every institutional line

All three — the Annual Package, Acharya and the Academy — earn 5% YSP + 0.5% RSM (Goutam, 23 Nov 2026: "all institutional at same commission rates"; R109.6, matching KMM — "School (5%) and Academy (5%) lines", "YSP 5% and RSM 0.5% of net"). Acharya is deliberately included: it is a scan, but it is sold to a school, so it takes the institutional rate. The direct scan's 7% is a different channel and is untouched — it is what a family pays for its own scan. A ₹15,000 package line therefore earns ₹750 / ₹75, and the package's embedded scan does not earn the scan rate separately — nothing records a component price to split it against.

What stays frozen

One catalogue, one price authority, no per-school SKU, no storefront exposure, and the invariant that a school's people never enter crm_families. Products, prices, component schedules, caps and rates are dated config versions with a proposer and an approver (R31, R43, R107.5, R109.8).

Money, seats and credit — the order things happen in

The school negotiates a price and a number of seats on its offer. A referred family pays for its own scan through the link; a batch's seats are bought by the school and its people are delivered, never checkout. Each payment is attributed once and paid to two people. A start — not a signature — is the sale.

1 · The offer: a line per product, a negotiated price and its own seats

There is no per-school SKU. A catalogue product (R109) is sold at a price negotiated with one school, for an agreed number of seats, and resolved by the one price authority only when the buyer arrived through that school's link (R105.6(a); Price::forInstitution). An offer may carry several product lines — each with its own price and seat cap — and every line closes independently. A direct buyer never sees it, and one school's offer is never another's. The price is the same for every family of that school: a running band where two parents of one school pay different amounts depending on when they buy is refused.

The negotiated "₹4,000 + GST" is stored as its GST-inclusive gross, ₹4,720, so the existing quote, cap, grace and capture gate apply unchanged. Each paid redemption takes one seat. When the agreed seats are gone the institution's link closes: it says the offer has ended and asks the school for a new batch — it never silently falls back to the regular price.

What is built and what is not. The paragraph above is the built path: a referred family paying through the school's link, one payment, one redemption. A school-paid batch — the ₹15,000 package in three instalments (D17), or an Acharya/Academy batch — has no money path today: there is no invoice, no instalment row, no partial-payment record and no collection reminder. Its design is written in institution billing (R110): agreement, schedule, tax invoice, recorded collections, commission per collection, aging, reminders and reconciliation, in three slices. Until slice A is built and the Finance rulings in that page are answered, no dated catalogue version sells an institutional line.

2 · The link: one model, two names on it

An institution's referral link is a campaign_links row whose partner is the institution instead of a person's partner ref, and it names the two designated posts that own its sales — the YSP and the RSM (R105.3). Because the designation lives on the link, a payment can never be attributed to an institution without also naming who earns. /go/{slug}, its utm_id stamp and the QR drawn from the same URL are unchanged; a link is deactivated, never edited, and its history stays.

Only the HO line creates one (today: campaign_link.create_hq). Both designated posts must be live, filled posts of the right type — a link that names a vacant or wrong-role post would pay nobody and is refused.

3 · The payment: attribution is automatic, and written once

A person who arrives through the link and pays — a referred family buying its own scan — is attributed automatically, no manual step (R105.4). A school's batch does not run on this path: the school pays for its seats, and the participants are delivery, never checkouts (R109.4–R109.6).

The payment path already writes the conversion row (kind = payment) from the utm_id; the append-only stamp is where the link's referrer institution is recorded, beside the selling post's credit. The designated YSP is the stamp's attributed/credited post; the designated RSM is stamped in its own network pair and resolved to whoever holds that post at the moment of payment — exactly as an attributed post is.

The family is found or created by the existing claim path. The institution is a tag on the sale, never an owner of the family, so ownership, vacancy credit and target credit do not move (R26, R78.3, R99).

4 · The ledger: two lines from one rate source

Every institutional payment credits two commission lines, each at the earner's own defined rule, from the one rule source (R6, R25, R105.5):

The table's 7% is today's rule for the scan family, which is what the built seam prices: a referred family buying its own scan. The institutional families — package, Acharya, Academy — carry their own configured rows once applied; the rate is config keyed by payee_role + product_family, never code (R109.6). All three take the same 5% YSP / 0.5% RSM; the direct scan's 7% stays its own, because it is a different channel. The RSM's 0.5% network line is identical on every line.

The school is not paid at the pilot

The attribution is reporting, not money (R105.5). No institution earns commission, and no institution login or payee exists. If an institutional payee is ever wanted, it is a payee_role on the existing rules keyed payee_role + product_family, implemented in the backend — the stamp already names the institution and both earners, so nothing would be re-attributed or backfilled. The recorded 5% (RSM 0.5%) for a future institution payee (D51, Q12) is untouched.

When a sale counts — the rule that does not change

A sale is a participant who submits their first task, in both funnels (R107.1). For a school that means per participant: a signed agreement, an active deal or a paid seat is operational progress and is not a sale. Payments awaiting a start are shown openly as "awaiting start" — the same "no silent zeros" discipline as the family funnel (R99.8). The commission follows the same unit: per participant payment, never a deal-level lump (R107.2) — for a person who pays their own way. An institutional line is a batch: its commission accrues on money collected against the offer line (D17, R109.6), and a participant never pays for it.

5 · From money to a student — the batch request and the handoff

Money unlocks a seat; the student's onboarding starts it (R111.1). The two clocks are kept apart on purpose: a settled instalment names the places the school bought, and a place starts only when the student's unit is minted at the end of onboarding — never at the signature, the invoice, the collection, the roster or the batch's planned date.

The seat is a record. It starts unlocked (money has arrived, no name yet), becomes assigned when a candidate is named, started at the unit's day 0, and ended with a reason — completed, not paid, institution ended or no-show. A seat still assigned 7 days after its batch's planned date returns to the roster: no refund, the place can be refilled once the original placement is withdrawn, and it never counts as a sale (R111.5).

The handoff does not change who provisions. The admin batch tool keeps its rules — challenge, Yashodarshi mentor, start date, funding, institution, a roster carrying the batch's required details, attested consent. The CRM adds the linked request and the write-back, not a second way to make a batch (R111.3). A provisioned batch continues through grace; a pause stops Godparent and sends no new request; a termination cancels an unprovisioned request, ends live seats as not paid and revokes the line's platform access (R111.7).

Two lists, on purpose

The joining list (R108) is the candidate roster — name and class, a lead list. The handoff list is the same people with everything the batch tool consumes: a valid email (login and the child picker are email-keyed), a DOB (the school's §9 attestation cannot cover a child whose age is unknown), and for a minor a guardian's name and relationship; phone, guardian contact and funding are validated when present. The request is refused whole — named row errors, nothing written — until every candidate is complete, and the desk's tool re-validates every row (R111.3). The sales side never creates a student or a crm_family from either list (R105.2).

The roster — one upload, two audiences

A school does not hand over its students one by one. It hands over a spreadsheet. That spreadsheet travels through the existing joining-list utility with an institution tag — not through a second importer (R108).

What is sent

The same endpoint and the same action as today (POST /crm/families/joining-list → ImportJoiningList) gain one optional field, institution_id. Absent, the direct joining list is unchanged. Present, the same rows are processed as that school's list: the idempotency checksum covers the tag, so the same file under two schools is two imports and the same file twice under one school returns the stored report (R108.2).

The template's minimum fields follow the offering, and never more (R108.7, R108.13):

School's offeringParticipantMinimum fieldsWhere the row lands
Child programmeA childname + classA family lead (the parent's contact), in crm_families
Acharya — a teacher-strengthening programmeA teacher (an adult)name + the school's own student_refThe institution's people — never crm_families (R105.2)

No address, no date of birth, no marks. A school's list is a lead list, not consent: nothing is sent to anyone before the family's own consent (R108.7).

Who may upload, and as whom

A tagged file needs institutions.import_students, granted to the school's two designated people and refused to everyone else. The action also checks the designation itself, so the ability alone can never file one school's list under another (R108.3). Whether the YSP or the RSM uploads, ownership, territory checks, unit, region and the claim all run as the designated YSP — an RSM's upload creates no RSM-owned family.

What each row does, and what it does not

Rows clear the same checks in the same order as today — invalid → do-not-contact → outside area → duplicate in file → identity match → claim — with one addition: each claimed family gains an append-only tag (family, institution, import, uploading post). The tag is a tag: not ownership, not consent, not a second family store. An already_yours family may be tagged; an owned_by_other family is not — no upload lets one post claim another post's family.

An uploaded student is not a sale, a payment, a seat or consent. They become sales only when each participant starts, and that start counts once, on the institution's line (R107.4, R108.5). Tagged rows carry source = institution_list, so they never count toward the direct joining list's 500; the cap exemption applies for the same reason — a school's list is not grabbing (R108.6).

One identity, two lists — and one reference that is the school's own

De-duplication stays exactly one map: the normalised contact hash in family_identifiers, shared by direct adds, both upload variants and payments. A child is not the identity: names and classes are never matched fuzzily. Inside one institution's file the school may supply its own admission or roll number as student_ref; the same institution + the same reference on a later upload is already_listed, changes nothing, and survives a changed parent number. The reference is stored on the tag and never matches across institutions (R108.11).

When two rules meet, the order is fixed

Conflict resolution is one ordered setting, not guesswork (R108.10, R108.12):

StepRuleConsequence
1ConsentA suppressed number is never claimed or tagged, whatever the file.
2OwnerThe owner keeps the family; neither an upload nor a link transfers it.
3StampA start already recorded is never reclassified.
4Link on this paymentOne payment carries exactly one utm_id, so one link decides that start's funnel and earners.
5TagA roster entry with no sale behind it is a tag only.

So: a family owned by another post is not tagged, yet if it later starts through the institution's link that start is still the institution's sale — the owner keeps the household, the school gets the sale it drove. A completed direct sale is never reclassified. A refund reverses the line it was paid on and never the other funnel's (R108.10(a)–(d)).

Today's rules are defaults, not constants

The conflict order, the institution-list switch, the source code, the template's fields and the cap exemption are all dated config versions (R108.12, R107.5). Each step's behaviour is one policy class — the single place to change it. The shape stays reverse-able too: institution_id is nullable, the tag is append-only, the institution list is a source value, and no funnel category is written onto crm_families — so a later reversal costs a config version, not a data rewrite.

One school, end to end

A concrete walk through the seam, in the order it happens. The example is a school buying a 200-seat batch of a child programme whose families pay through its link — the money path that is built. Where the school pays for the batch by invoice (the Acharya and Academy shape), the same ladder runs with the invoice in place of the checkout; that path is designed in R110 (institution billing).

Week 1 · visit
The YSP finds the schoolThe YSP records the visit as an institutional activity — subject: the school. The deal moves prospect → contacted → needs_visit. Nobody's family pipeline moves, and no child's data exists anywhere yet.
Week 2 · proposal
Price per seat, seat countThe proposal is one price for 200 seats, stored GST-inclusive. The GM/CEO approve the offer as configuration (D17). The deal sits at proposal, then negotiation. No commission, no target credit, no sale.
Week 4 · agreement
The school signsThe RSM approves the agreement. The deal moves to agreement. Still no sale: a signature is progress (R107.1).
Week 4 · onboarding
Two posts named, offer activatedThe in-app onboarding flow creates the institution account, names the designated YSP and RSM, and activates the offer (R106.7). The deal is at onboarding. The HO line mints the institution's link for the governed destination.
Week 5 · roster
The school's teacher list arrivesThe YSP (or the RSM) uploads one spreadsheet through the joining-list utility with institution_id. Each row lands as the institution's person — not a family record — with the tag recording the school, the import and the school's own student_ref. Owners are unchanged; no consent is implied; no sale is counted.
Week 5 · link
The link reaches the familiesThe school's link carries the negotiated offer and names the two earners. A parent opens /go/{code}; the click is counted; the offer's price is resolved for this school only.
Week 6 · first payment
The first family pays, and the deal goes activeThe payment writes a payment conversion and one append-only stamp: referrer institution, attributed YSP, network RSM. Two commission lines accrue at their own rules. One seat is taken; the first paid redemption moves the deal to active.
Week 7 · first task
The student starts — now it is a saleThe participant submits their first task. That is the institution's sale, once, on the institution's line (R107.4). It increments the institutional target, not the family target, and the digest shows it in the institutional section.
Month 3 · last seat
The batch sells outThe 200th paid redemption takes the last seat. The link closes with "offer ended" and the deal moves to renewal_due. The school agrees a new batch — a new offer and a new link — and the ladder starts again from a position of strength.
The batch shape differs at weeks 5–7

An Acharya or Academy batch never has a participant checkout: the school pays for its seats by invoice, and each realised first instalment unlocks its seat — paid seats start in the next weekly delivery batch (Sunday by default; typically 30–50, up to 1,000, whatever was paid). The roster is only the candidate list; the payment starts the seat. For the annual package, the scan starts on instalment 1 and the Godparent on instalment 2 — and the dates never move: a late payment shortens the months delivered rather than shifting the anniversary. Late money is not cut off on the due date: a 30–60 day grace window keeps the package alive — an unpaid instalment 2 leaves Godparent unstarted, and once started the grace keeps it running (D70); after the window the package pauses, and 120 days past due it is terminated as not paid. The commission accrues on the money collected. Weeks 5–7 above describe the referred-family path that is built today (R109.4–R109.6, R110).

What the school never becomes

Not the owner of a household, not a payee, not a consent holder, not a second CRM. It is an account with a ladder, a link, an offer and a roster — and the payments it drives are attributed to two named people who earn.

Family direct and institutional, side by side

The differences are real, and they are few. Everything not on this list is the same machinery, which is why the two funnels do not drift.

The same under the hood — the list that must not grow

One price authority

List price and institutional offer are resolved by the same code; the institution is a parameter, not a second price list.

One attribution stamp

Written once at payment for both funnels; the institution is an extra column, not an extra process.

One commission engine

Two lines are two calls at two rules; no institutional formula exists.

One stage engine

Two configured ladders over cfg_stages and cfg_stage_transitions.

One target engine

A funnel dimension added to level × scope × period; pace and nudges stay single.

One importer

The joining list, with or without an institution tag.

One identity map

family_identifiers, shared by every way a contact can arrive.

One link model

campaign_links and /go; the institution is a partner type, not a link type.

One activity record

Exactly one subject, with the same counters, heat and next action as a family.

One audit trail

Institutional moves and link creation are audited like every other CRM action.

What each role sees

The screens follow the abstractions: a lens switch, not a second application. Their detailed design comes with the build; this is the contract they must satisfy.

Two lenses, one product (R107.6)

Leadership screens — RSM, GM, HO desk, CEO — show Families | Institutions as tabs. The field Today screen shows the same two lenses as a switch that filters in place, because a YSP works both in one day. The daily digest stays one email per person, split into two sections; there is no second email engine and no third nudge. The two-nudge rule and the merged day plan are unchanged.

Reports: two pipelines, two denominators (R106.8)

An institution → link → lead, registration and payment → paid sales is a GROUP BY over the indexed conversion rows and the attribution index (R105.7). A referred family counts in the family funnel; the deal it belongs to counts in the institutional funnel; no sale is counted in both. Seats sold and participants enrolled are measures under a deal, never pipeline rows of their own. An institutional report carries counts, amounts and the institution's name only.

Why this order

The link, price, stamp and money had to exist first: they are the parts a payment touches, and no part of the institutional build ships before the payments and attribution seam is solved (R105). The roster, pipeline and targets sit on top of that seam and change no family rule.

Change a number or a list, not a release

Stages and transitions, targets and their funnel, outcome and miss-reason lists, digest sections, day windows, the upload's rows and template fields, the institution-list switch and the conflict order are each a governed, dated version applied by crm:config:apply (R31, R43, R107.5, R108.8, R108.12). Code reads configuration; a new stage, reason, target row or digest section is a config change. The abilities that expose each institutional screen live in the access config like every other ability — and a field with no mask row is not sent, by design.

The only things deliberately frozen

The invariant (R105.2), the one-sale rule (R107.1), the append-only stamp and the one identity map are not knobs. Everything else is a dated version with a proposer and an approver, and a reversal costs configuration — not a data rewrite.
