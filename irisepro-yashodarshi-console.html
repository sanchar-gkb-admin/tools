Why sorted by risk, never by name

The list exists to answer one question — who needs me right now. Alphabetical order answers a question nobody asks. Healthy evaluators collapse to a single quiet line.

Two actions, and only two

Nudge sends the evaluator the deadline email — the missing last hop, since today both the warning and the breach go only to ops via OpsAlert and the evaluator is never told.

Reassign is the same re-allocation EvaluationsSlaScan already performs automatically on breach — made available before the breach, by a human who can see it coming.

Not on this screen: grading. Admin monitors; the Yashodarshi evaluates. Putting a score box here would put an unqualified marker one click from a learner's result.

05 The two clocks — and what the Chief Yashodarshi watches

Added 26 Aug 2026. This console carried one "Median" for a year, and it was the wrong one to plan with. There are two clocks and they answer different questions.

ColumnQuestion it answersWho it is about
WaitHow long did the learner sit there before anyone looked?the learner's experience — this is the SLA
Hands-onHow long did the evaluator actually spend on this task?the evaluator's effort — this is capacity

Why it matters. Before this, the only per-task figure in the whole system was a guess — 90 seconds of watching plus 22.5 seconds per ability, about 12.75 minutes. Nobody had ever timed it. Measured against real evaluations it was roughly four times too high, and it was keyed on ability count rather than on submission type, which is what actually moves the number: a video takes about 2.2× an audio.

Idle is excluded at source. Hands-on time accumulates only while heartbeats keep arriving. Step away for twenty minutes and the returning beat adds nothing — so a cockpit left open on a second monitor is not counted as marking. There is no outlier rule to argue about afterwards.

What the Chief Yashodarshi looks for

PatternWhat it usually meansWhat to do
Hands-on well BELOW the team median
e.g. 1.1 min against a 3.4 min norm
 Not speed — usually the rubric is being skimmed. Thirty ability dials cannot be judged in a minute. Read two of their scorecards yourself before congratulating anyone on throughput.
Hands-on well ABOVE it Either genuinely hard submissions, or someone who has not been shown the fast path through the cockpit. Sit with them once. This is a coaching signal, not a performance one.
Wait high, hands-on normal A queue problem, not a person problem. They are marking at a fine pace; the work reached them late or in a burst. Look at allocation, not the evaluator.
Wait normal, hands-on rising over weeks Fatigue, or the task mix has shifted toward video. Check the mode split before assuming it is the person.
Hands-on shows — Fewer than 10 sittings recorded. NOT zero, and not "instant". Nothing. Wait for evidence. A number built on three sittings is how the last guess survived a year.

One caution. Hands-on is a measure of time, not of quality. It tells you where to look, never what to conclude. Every row above ends in "go and read the work" for that reason.

One thing to measure before building

Is on_time actually populated in prod? EvalSla returns null whenever the clock start can't be resolved — a 7-task set still in window, a missing deadline — and the row stamps null.

If most rows are null, the two history columns are empty and the screen loses half its value. One count settles it, and it should be run before the screen is built, not discovered on it.

Why this matters beyond today's incident

27 Acharyas, 300 units, a 12-hour per-task SLA. "Which evaluators are keeping up?" is a weekly question with no screen that answers it.

It also decides allocation: only senior evaluators may take ₹4999 work (abort_unless($y->isSenior(), 403)), so knowing which seniors are reliable is the difference between a smooth cohort close and another Nayan M.
