YASHHO · Storyboard · v2

The board — capturing transformation without asking anyone to describe it

Ask a learner how they changed and you get “it was good, I learnt a lot.” Not because nothing happened — because noticing is the hard part, and a fourteen-year-old has no reason to have done it. This is the instrument that does the noticing for them, what it produces, and when to ask.

Open the working prototype →

The one idea everything follows from. YASHHO already holds the evidence: the scorecard, the AACE deltas, the task history, the Yashodarshi’s own words. So we never ask “how did you change?” — we show them what changed and ask them to react. Reacting is easy. Remembering is not.
01

The board — one spine, four skins

Five lines, five minutes. It is not a testimonial form; it is a mini vision board for what someone stands for, in their words. We supply the grammar, never the content.

★The moment — something that already happened. The only line here with a fact in it.
1The stand — I am someone who ___ (present tense, not aspiration)
2The cost — which means I’ll stop ___
3The witness — a named person, never “everyone”
4The date
5The recovery — when I don’t, I’ll ___
Why a stand and not a vision. A vision board is about having — the rank, the college, the car. Nothing on it can fail, so nobody is accountable to it, and it is the affirmation tradition that breaks under any honest look at your own life. A stand is public by construction, costs something, and can be broken. Line 2 is what separates a stand from a wish: if they can’t name what it costs, they’ve expressed a preference.
Line 5 is the most valuable thing in the product, and no vision board has it. Integrity isn’t never breaking your word — it’s what you do the moment you notice you have. A board with no failure mode dies on its first failure and the person walks away carrying shame instead of a practice. Teaching a thirteen-year-old that a broken word is recoverable may outlast everything else here.
02

Four archetypes — same shape, different words

The archetype is derived, never asked — stream, tier, acharya_*, account role. What changes per skin is vocabulary, who counts as a witness, how far “by when” reaches, and one archetype-specific line.

Keep the shape identical. A parent has to recognise their child’s board in an aspirant’s board — that recognition is what makes a fifteen-year hypothesis feel purchasable today. Four separate designs would destroy the only property that makes cross-population proof work.

🎒 The learner · Class 6–10

★Was there a task you nearly didn’t finish?
1I’m someone who ___
2Which means I’ll stop ___
3Who’ll notice first? class teacher · friend · Amma · Appa · sibling
4By ___ this week · this month · before my next scorecard
5If I don’t, I’ll tell ___ and say why

Horizon is this week — a thirteen-year-old cannot hold December. Line 3 forces a name, which is the guard against “I stand for honesty and hard work.”

📚 The aspirant · KAS / IAS

★“Why has it taken this long?” — answer it now, as a stand rather than a defence
1I’m the kind of candidate who ___
2Which means in the room I will not ___
3Who’ll hold me to it? study partner · mentor · spouse · the parent funding this
4By which mock / attempt?
5When I slip, I’ll return by ___

Tone is the design. These are adults who have failed publicly and will close the tab at the first whiff of a coaching-centre poster — no encouragement, no exclamation marks. The ★ is the question they dread most, and rehearsing it as a stand is the most useful line on any of these boards.

🪔 The Acharya · teacher

★A moment in your class this term you’ve thought about since.
1I’m a teacher who ___
2Which means I’ll stop ___ (something you do, not something you’ll assign)
3Which colleague will know?
4By the end of which term?
5When I forget, I’ll ___
+One child who’ll be different because of this — a name, not a type of child

The failure mode is a teacher converting the practice into homework, which kills it — hence line 2 asks them to give something up. The named child is where the multiplier lives.

🏠 The parent

★Something she said recently that you haven’t stopped thinking about.
1I’m a parent who ___
2Which means I’ll stop ___
3Your child. Fixed, not a picker — the only screen with no input
4By ___
5When I slip back, I’ll ___
+The complaint you keep making, said instead as a request

For a minor the parent is the default respondent, and their board ends with a handoff — “Ananya has her own board to fill. It’s hers — you won’t see it unless she shows you.” That privacy line is what makes the child answer honestly instead of performing for whoever is holding the phone.

03

What happened when we judged the output instead of the form

The form read beautifully. So we rendered what each board actually produces and asked one question of it: would a stranger forward this?

BoardWhat v1 producesVerdict
LearnerA scene and a commitmentpasses — only because of the ★ line
Aspirant“I was preparing to be right instead of preparing to be useful”a line, not a story — best 12-second card in the set
Acharya“the boy on the last bench who answers only when nobody is looking”best image, no action
ParentPromises onlynot publishable at v1
The ★ line was doing all the work — and it was last, and optional. Everything else on the board is future tense: admirable, and unshareable. The one line asking about something that already happened is the story. It now opens every form, is never optional, and asking it first also makes “I’m someone who…” easier to answer, which was the hardest cold open in the flow.
Three of four boards are not publishable at v1. They are excellent instruments for change and weak instruments for story. If we expect shares from all four in month one we will ship three weak ones and wrongly conclude the format doesn’t work. Month one’s shares come from learners. The other three become stories at T+30, in the diff — which makes the follow-up not an accountability nicety but where most of the material comes from.
04

The measure — and the metric that will lie to you

Two checks, one before anything is built and one after.

Pre-ship · the cold read

Show a rendered board to someone with no context — not staff, not a customer — and ask one question: “What did this person do?” If they can’t answer with a specific action, there’s no scene in it and it will not travel. This is the test that failed three of the four boards above, before a line of Laravel was written.

Post-ship · shared unasked

Not views. Not reads. Not completion. A share is a person putting their own name behind it — the only signal here that cannot be gamed from the inside.

Completion rate is the wrong metric and it will look like success. A form with 90% completion producing boards nobody forwards has failed. Worse, optimising for completion pushes the design shorter, easier and blander — and the first two casualties would be the recovery line and the scene line, which are the two carrying the whole thing. The acceptance criterion is not “learners finish it”. It is “a stranger reads one and forwards it.”
05

When to ask — the hooks

Trigger on task completion, not scorecard delivery. The scorecard can lag; the feeling cannot wait. Where the scorecard already exists, anchor on the AACE delta; where it doesn’t, anchor on the task they just finished.

T+0 · in-appThe moment the last task is submittedHighest yield by a distance. Everything after this is recovery.
T+1 · emailOnly if they bouncedSubject line is the mirror: “Your Execution went 41 → 72.”
T+3 · emailFinal ask, different angleThe moment, not the number. Then stop.
T+7Link expiresAfter this the CTA is log in.

Two reminders, never three. Suppress the instant a board exists. For minors the mail goes to the guardian on the account, not the child.

The magic link must be a capability token, not a login. The existing magic link mints a session — ~/login-link.sh hands over the account. A forwarded board email would then be seven days of full access to a minor’s scorecard. Same signed-URL machinery, different scope: opens one form, writes one board, single-use, reads nothing else, and surfaces only the delta it needs. On expiry it falls back to real login. This has to be a separate token type or the two get conflated the first time someone reuses the helper.
06

T+30 — the diff, and where the story actually is

Short. Two or three questions, under a minute. They already gave you a board; this is a check, not a second form.

Four rules that decide whether this produces truth or flattery.
① Quote their own words back — never “how’s it going?”
② Make “no” the easiest answer to give. If the honest answer is costly, you get politeness. The most valuable response here is “I slipped, and here’s what I did” — that is the recovery line being tested, which is the whole practice.
③ Ask the witness separately, and tell both people the other cannot see their answer.
④ The publishable artefact is the pair, not either half.

🎒 Learner

quote“Thirty days ago you said you’d stop writing long answers so nobody could tell you were unsure.”
1What actually happened? I did it · Mostly · I slipped, then went back · Not yet
2If did → What did you notice? · If slipped → What did you do when you noticed?
→To the witness: “Has Ananya done anything differently this month? You don’t have to be kind — she can’t see this.”

📚 Aspirant

quote“You said that in the room you would not pad an answer to fill the silence.”
1Have you been in a room where it was tested? Yes, and I held it · Yes, and I didn’t · Not yet
2What happened?
3“Say your ‘why has it taken this long’ answer out loud now. Has it changed?”
→To the study partner or mentor: “Has anything changed in how they work, or how they talk about this?”

Q3 is self-evidencing: if that answer has changed in thirty days, that is the transformation, in their own voice, with no interpretation from us.

🪔 Acharya

quote“You named the boy on the last bench who answers only when nobody is looking.”
1“Has he answered out loud yet?” Yes · Not yet · Something else happened
2Tell me about it.
3And what did you stop doing?
→To the colleague: “Have you noticed anything different in their class?”

The strongest single question in the system, and it writes itself out of what they already told us. A teacher will answer that one.

🏠 Parent

quote“You said you’d stop comparing her to her cousin.”
1How did it go? I did · Mostly · I slipped and said so · I didn’t manage it
2What happened when you slipped?
→To the child, privately: “Has anything changed at home this month?” — she won’t see your answer, you won’t see hers

And this is what it produces

What Lakshmi wrote in August:
“I’ll stop comparing her to her cousin. When I slip, I’ll say out loud that I did it again — in front of her.”

What Ananya said in September, asked separately:
“She said sorry. My mother said sorry to me. I didn’t know that was allowed.”

Neither of them wrote a testimonial. Two honest answers, thirty days apart, from two people who couldn’t see each other’s.

Publish the parent story in the child’s voice, not the parent’s. A parent describing their own growth reads as self-congratulation. A child reporting a change in their parent cannot be argued with — they have no reason to flatter.
Practical blocker to solve before this ships. If the only address on file is the guardian’s, the child’s “separately, privately” answer is not actually independent — it lands in the same inbox. For minors the child’s half has to be captured in-app, while logged in as the learner, not by email. Verify which contacts exist per household before designing the send.
07

Examples — grounded, and keyed to the right challenge

Two per question, behind a link, never shown by default. Two rather than one on purpose: a single example becomes a template — show one and half the cohort writes a variation of it. Two that disagree with each other give permission instead of a pattern.

They name tasks that actually exist, read from database/seeders/data/prod-catalog-mapping.tsv:

  • Learner (ch 10, Board Exam Booster, 21 tasks) — The Feedback Mirror, The Thank You Talk with your Parents
  • Aspirant (ch 54, KAS 21-task) — The Ethical Officer Test, You Are the Assistant Commissioner at 6:30 AM
  • Parent — the two mandatory parent-conversation tasks they were physically in the room for: The Thank You Talk, My Future: Their Dream, My Dream
  • Acharya (ch 63, Mission Acharya Class 5–10, 21 tasks) — Listening Without Defending: Responding to Unfiltered Student Feedback, 8 Quick Checks: Do My Students Really Understand?
The Acharya tasks exist — they are just not in the repo. Mission Acharya runs as three challenges in prod — 62 acharya_foundation (Pre-KG–4), 63 acharya_middle (5–10), 64 acharya_senior (PU/UG) — all tier 4999, audience=acharya, 21 tasks each. They carry no rows in prod-catalog-mapping.tsv, so anything reading the seed data alone concludes they don’t exist. Worth knowing for every future audit, not just this one: the seeded catalogue is not the catalogue.
And the Acharya briefs are unusually good raw material. “Listening Without Defending” asks a teacher to receive unfiltered student feedback without answering back; “8 Quick Checks” routinely shows them that a lesson they were sure had landed reached a third of the room. Both are already the scene the ★ line is fishing for — which is why the Acharya board needed a scene question rather than inventing one.
Key examples by challenge_id, not by archetype. Focus is the 21-task 4999 tier, but the 999 and 1999 tiers carry 5 and 7 tasks. Show a 7-task KAS aspirant an example about The Ethical Officer Test and they know instantly they’re reading someone else’s programme — which costs more trust than the example buys. Offer only tasks present in that learner’s own snapshot.
08

The share — 200 words, four beats

A teenager speaking conversationally runs 130–150 words a minute, and a testimonial is read slower than prose. The whole share is about 200 words — four beats, fifty words each. That is the spec, not a style note.

0:00 – 0:10The confessionOpen at the lowest point. No setup, no “I joined YASHHO in March.”
0:10 – 0:45One sceneOne task, one person, one thing that was said.
0:45 – 1:05The ordinary TuesdayWhat they do now — and who noticed without being asked.
1:05 – 1:30The declaration“I’m someone who ___,” with a date. Then the number. Then the logo.

“I used to write really long answers. Not because I knew more — because if I wrote enough, nobody could tell which part I wasn’t sure about.

There was a task about a decision I regretted. I wrote four pages, and my Yashodarshi sent back one line: which one sentence would you keep? I sat with that for two days. I was angry about it, actually.

Then I kept one sentence. It was the only true thing on the page.

Now I write that sentence first. In class, in my notebook, when my mother asks me something. Last month my class teacher asked me to stop putting my hand up so often, so others could go.

I’m someone who says the true thing first. By December I want to be the one they ask to open the debate.”

Read what she never says. Never “confident”, never “grateful”, never the programme’s name. The teacher noticing does work no self-description can — nobody can credibly report their own transformation. And it ends unfinished, on a December nobody has seen. That is the only line that makes a stranger want to come back.

Six rules, ordered by what they cost when broken

  • Open on a confession, not a claim. “I wrote long answers so nobody could tell” stops a scroll. “YASHHO changed my life” is wallpaper.
  • Someone else must notice. Without the third party, every other beat reads as a claim.
  • A number, not a superlative. 41 → 72 costs four characters and does what “improved a lot” cannot.
  • One scene, not a summary. Ten weeks in a paragraph is a brochure. One Tuesday is a story.
  • End on an open loop. A declaration with a date is the click — not the CTA.
  • The product appears in the last five seconds. The story sells; the logo says where to go.

10

Reels — same capture, no second process

Add “record instead of type” to the same screens and the reel is already shot, in order, at the right length.

Do not film it. UGC works because it looks unproduced. The moment there is a crew, a backdrop or a ring light, the viewer reclassifies it as an advertisement and stops believing the number. The learner’s own front camera is not a compromise; it is the format.

0:30 · the confession

The ★ answer alone. Hook lands inside 1.5 seconds.

0:45 · the noticing

Learner intercut with the person who noticed. Strongest, hardest to get.

0:15 · the declaration

Line 1 over the delta. Cheapest, highest cadence — one a week.

1:30 · the arc

All four beats, one face, one cut. Two or three a year.

  • Vertical, front camera, one take, 30 seconds. A retake button, not an edit timeline — the second take is always better and the fourth is always worse.
  • Prompt over the viewfinder, so they answer a question instead of performing to a lens.
  • No script. The instant a child reads, the eyes go dead and everything after reads as coached.
  • Captions are not optional — most of this is watched on mute.
  • The only graphic is their own number. No app screenshots, no music that tells you how to feel.
  • Under-13s: the parent holds the phone and answers. Often the better film — the noticing comes from outside the child, and nobody watches a small person perform.

Two a week beats twenty in a month. Reels compound on cadence, not polish — which is the argument for building this into the product rather than commissioning it. The ask has to sit where the feeling already is.

11

Consent

Given YASHHO’s existing posture — payment-corroborated guardian consent, erasure that anonymises in place and keeps the ledger — a child’s words on a public page is a separate consent from the one given at enrolment, and it should look and feel separate.

  • The ask must never read as a condition of the programme, or of getting the scorecard.
  • Default to first name only. Full name and class is an opt-up.
  • A face is not a sentence. Consent to a written quote is not consent to a reel — same ladder, asked again, and the screen must name the format: “a video of you, on Instagram and YouTube.”
  • Withdrawal is one tap, no reason required, and it must actually unpublish — so the admin holds the destination list, not just the file.
A story taken without full consent is worth less than no story. It is the one asset that turns from proof into liability the moment anyone asks how it was obtained.
12

The Yashodarshi collects it — admin is the backstop

At 4999 each seat carries a dedicated evaluator, and the relationship is already months old. A request from the person who has read every one of your twenty-one submissions is a different object entirely from a system email.

And they can personalise the one question that matters. The Yashodarshi wrote the feedback on every task — they know exactly which one the learner nearly abandoned. So the ★ line stops being a generic prompt and becomes “You almost didn’t finish the Feedback Mirror, did you? Tell me about that.” Nobody else in the system can ask it that way, and that phrasing is worth more than any amount of copy tuning.

It belongs on the existing mentee surface — /yashodarshi/mentorship and /yashodarshi/mentee/{unit}/scorecard — as the same three answers, scoped to their own students: who is outstanding, who has breached 72 hours, and a link I can send right now.

Measured in prod, and it changes the plan: 24 of 64 active 4999 units (37%) have no yashodarshi_id at all. Completed units run the same — 60 of 172. So a Yashodarshi-led channel alone would silently miss about one seat in three, in exactly the tier it was designed for. The admin fallback is not an edge case; it is a third of the work.

Worth a separate look, and larger than this feature: those 24 active seats are paying for dedicated evaluation. The null may be legitimate — pending allocation, demo or holding-queue units — or it may be Unit’s own fail-safe guard having nulled a bad assignment and nobody noticing. Cause not established here; do not assume either.
The Yashodarshi may collect the board. They must never collect the satisfaction. The board is the learner’s own stand — asking for it is a coaching act, and the relationship makes the answer better. Anything that reads as “how did I do?” inverts that: the learner will be kind, and kindness is worthless data. For the same reason, the T+30 witness question must never be routed through the Yashodarshi when the change being reported touches their own impact.

The SLA clock starts at task completion, not at scorecard delivery — same trigger as the automated nudge, or the two will disagree and the queue will show phantom breaches whenever a scorecard lags.

The copy-paste link

One click puts a ready URL on the clipboard, with its expiry shown next to it, so staff can drop it into WhatsApp without leaving the screen. Which raises the thing that has to be got right:

An admin-generated link is an account-access vector unless it is scoped. It must be the same capability token as the emailed one — opens one form, writes one board, reads nothing else, single-use, short expiry. Never a session. And because copy-paste means WhatsApp, assume every link gets forwarded: single-use plus a short window is what makes a forwarded link already dead rather than a door into a minor’s scorecard. Log who minted it, for whom, and when — an admin who can silently generate access to any learner is a hole regardless of intent.
Don’t let “breached” become a staff scoreboard. It is a work queue — a list of people to ring — not a performance metric. The moment it is reported upward as a percentage, the incentive shifts to closing rows rather than getting honest boards, and a chased board is worth less than none.

Plus Excel export of the same view for offline processing, and the T+30 queue as a second tab against the same shape — the follow-up needs exactly the same three answers.

The weekly measure

A standing weekly report: everyone who completed their assessment, passed 72 hours, and still has no board. Same list, scoped to their own mapped students, on the Yashodarshi dashboard.

SectionWhat it showsWhy it’s there

The dashboard detects; the weekly report holds people to it. Don’t let the weekly rhythm become the SLA — if breaches only surface on Mondays, the real window quietly becomes 72 hours plus up to seven days. The Yashodarshi and admin views must be live and continuous; the report is the governance beat on top, not the detection mechanism.
And it needs a stop rule. After two or three weeks outstanding, drop the row rather than chase it forever. A board extracted at day forty, under pressure, from someone who has already declined twice is worth less than no board — and it will fail the cold-read test anyway. Let it go and put the effort into the next cohort, where the feeling is still live.
One query, three surfaces. The weekly report, the admin band and the Yashodarshi list are the same question asked with different scopes. They must come from a single resolver — if the report and the dashboard ever disagree about who is breached, nobody will trust either, and the argument about which is right will cost more than the feature saved.

Where it goes — the Cohort Scoreboard, not the student list

This should be a band on reports/cohort-scoreboard (CohortScoreboardController), not a new screen and not the student index.

  • The SLA is inherently time-windowed, and a batch finishes together. “Who is outstanding right now” only means something against a cohort. On the global student list the same column is permanently noisy and sorted by nothing useful.
  • That screen already answers “who has done what” — students as columns, filling as each task is evaluated. Board-submitted is the same question in one more column, next to the completion it depends on.
  • It is already batch-scoped and institution-filterable (cohort = challenge + start date), which is exactly the slice someone chasing boards works in.
  • Everything there is derived via CohortInsightService, with nothing hardcoded. Board state should be derived the same way rather than denormalised into a new column.
But the copy-link action belongs on the student row in both places. When a parent rings up you look them up by name, not by cohort — so the button has to exist on the student record too. Which means the SLA state must be computed in one place (a single BoardSla-style resolver) and rendered by both surfaces. Two independent implementations of “is this breached?” is precisely the defect class that produced StudentSeat, ScorecardDelivery and LeaderboardService — each built reactively after the drift had already cost something.
14

Build order

  1. Board v1 at task completion — four skins, the ★ line first. This is the only piece with a real cost to delay: you cannot retroactively capture who someone was before they changed.
  2. The share render — prose + card image. The reward, and the distribution.
  3. The T+30 check, including the witness half. Where most of the material comes from.
  4. The carousel renderer — deterministic, weekly cadence from day one.
  5. The Yashodarshi list — outstanding + breached, scoped to their own mentees, on the existing mentorship surface. At 4999 this is the primary channel and among the cheapest things here to build, because the relationship already exists.
  6. Admin + the weekly report — the same resolver at a wider scope: the Cohort Scoreboard band, the copy-paste link, Excel export, and cover for the ~37% of active 4999 seats with no evaluator. Needs the capability token first.
  7. Reels — last. They need willing humans.
Before any of it: run ten by hand this week. Open a learner’s scorecard, find the biggest AACE delta and the sharpest Yashodarshi line, ring them, ask the six questions in order. Fifteen minutes each. You will learn which questions actually open people up — and it will not be all six.
