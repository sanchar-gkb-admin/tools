Instant demo result — without a Yashodarshi

The demo task hooks a lead — but a lead wants a result now, and our real evaluation is a human Yashodarshi (up to 12h per task). Here's how we give an honest instant result for a paisa, keep the human eval as the paid product, and never put unpaid leads on our evaluators.

The two paths — and why we do both

Path A

Human Yashodarshi, 72h SLA

  • Real, deep — but no instant result (72h wait).
  • Doesn't scale: every lead = a YD task; evaluators are for paying students.
  • Fine for a trickle, collapses on funnel volume.
Path B — recommended

Instant machine snapshot + human call

  • Machine gives the instant taste (a paisa, no wait).
  • The mentor call (2–5 min) is the real close.
  • Scales infinitely · YDs untouched · moat intact.

How we keep it honest (and protect the moat)

Narrow: Communication only — what a clip can truly show.
Framed a "snapshot," never a final score — so the deep human eval differing is expected.
Objective where possible: pace/energy are measured, not guessed — can't be wrong.
Human eval stays the product — the demo is a taste, not a second scoring engine.

The bigger prize — can AI match a human evaluator?

🎯 This demo is R&D disguised as marketing. Evaluator labour is our single biggest cost. Every free demo quietly tests "can AI score like a Yashodarshi?" on the easiest pillar — funding itself with leads while building the proof. If it matches, it's the biggest margin lever we have.
AI already wins

Where AI matches or beats a human

  • Consistency — two humans (or the same human, tired) score differently; AI never drifts.
  • Micro-measurement — it measures pace, pauses, vocabulary, structure a human only intuits.
  • Evidence — cites the exact quote/moment behind every score. Auditable.
  • Scale + cost — instant, a paisa, infinite.
Human still wins

Where the human still leads

  • Holistic judgment — sincerity, the spark, a creative answer off the rubric.
  • Catching gaming — a coached or memorised submission.
  • Trust — "a real coach assessed my child" > "an AI scored it." A perception moat.

How we make AI match — the method (not "trust the AI")

1 · Calibrate against our own data. We already have thousands of Yashodarshi-scored tasks. Run AI on the same, measure agreement per ability — a real number, not a hope.
2 · The rubric is the bridge. AI eval fails on vague rubrics; ours is precise (0–5 bands, descriptors, benchmarks). The same thing that keeps YDs consistent makes AI trainable — our unfair advantage.
3 · Split the 64 by measurability. Objective (pace, grammar, vocabulary) → AI matches/beats. Inference (confidence, empathy) → AI approaches. Deeply contextual → keep the human.
4 · Confidence + human spot-check. AI scores + a confidence. High → automatic. Low/borderline → a YD reviews. Matches human reliability, and cuts YD time 5–10× (they check the hard 15%, not all 100%).
5 · The demo proves it safely. Low-stakes (unpaid lead), Communication-only (most measurable) — validate "does AI match the human?" before it ever touches a paying student's score.
🛡️ Augment, don't replace. AI does the first pass; the human stays on the hard cases and the trust. We only expand AI's autonomy ability-by-ability as the numbers earn it — protecting the moat while transforming the cost.
